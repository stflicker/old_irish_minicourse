02 / FOCUS

The rule, clearly

Read the forms: function → change

W2a leicid : ní·leici and W2b roithid : ní·roithi exhibit the same third-singular ending contrast. Compare each verb's own forms before comparing the subclasses.

Unlike a W1/W2 comparison, W2a and W2b do not differ in their present ending sets. Stifter's diagnostic is the short root vowel o or u in W2b. W2a covers the other W2 verbs.

The historical background helps explain the split: many W2b verbs come from old causative/iterative formations. Roithid means ‘makes run’ and is historically related to reithid ‘runs’. W2a includes many denominative verbs formed from nouns/adjectives.

Takeaway: Classifying a verb by its conjunct -i tells you W2, but not whether it is W2a or W2b. For that, inspect the root vowel and source paradigm.

Source: Stifter (2006), §12.1, esp. discussion of W2a/W2b and note 4; Thurneysen §§336–337.

04 / HISTORICAL MORPHOLOGY

PIE → Celtic → Old Irish

Historical analysis and sources (tap to collapse)

1. Two distinct PIE derivational pathways can converge

The W2 class is divided into W2a and W2b primarily by historical stem formation. Both subclasses use the same Old Irish W2 finite endings, which is precisely why a one-form test is inadequate.

1. Two distinct PIE derivational pathways can converge
Type Older morphological association Old Irish model
W2a denominative/related -eyé- derivatives leicid
W2b causative/iterative type often involving **o-grade root + accented *-éye/o-*** roithid ‘makes run’

The division is a comparative classification, not a claim that Old Irish speakers consciously formed every W2b verb by PIE ablaut.

2. Where the o came from

In some Indo-European causative/iterative formations, a root appears with o-grade instead of an e-grade found in a related basic verb. The Irish pair reithid ‘runs’ versus roithid ‘makes run’ is a memorable illustration of a formal and semantic relationship. Celtic sound changes alter the pronunciation, and later analogy can obscure the prehistoric derivation.

3. Major morphological innovation

The distinct old derivational semantics became partly lexicalized: W2b verbs are not automatically interpretable as causatives in every Old Irish text, and W2a verbs are not automatically productive denominatives. At the same time, finite inflection was leveled across both subclasses. That is a clear example of old stem-forming categories surviving as morphological subclasses after their original formation rules lose transparency.

References: Stifter (2006), §12.1; Le Mair, Secondary Verbs in Old Irish.
