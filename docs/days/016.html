L / N / H / 0 - how to read the following-word markers

Following-word effects: L lenition; N nasalization; H historical aspiration/gemination; 0 no regular L/N/H effect prescribed here. The marker belongs to the form before it and describes the next closely connected word, not a suffix. Effects need not be visible in spelling; phonological and syntactic restrictions still apply. Prepositional = the case often called dative. The vocative particle a itself lenites the noun; a marker at the end of the vocative phrase describes what follows the noun.

02 / FOCUS

The rule, clearly

Unlike masculine o-stems, feminine a-stem nominative and accusative plural are generally identical: tuatha in both functions. The prepositional plural is readily recognized as tuathaib.

The old a-stem label refers to a historical stem vowel. Modern-looking case endings need not preserve a visible a in every form.

Why look at cell? It shows that the case pattern interacts with root-vowel raising in particular lexical items. The genitive singular cille does not resemble a simple string added onto the nominative cell.

Try to produce two forms without looking: plural genitive of tuath and singular genitive of cell.

Source: Stifter (2006), §§10.1–10.2.

04 / HISTORICAL MORPHOLOGY

PIE → Celtic → Old Irish

Extended analysis of inheritance, sound change, analogy and innovations · optional 5–8 minutes

Historical analysis and sources (tap to collapse)

1. PIE feminine plurals were not all identical

Many reconstructed PIE -eh₂-stem paradigms distinguished nominative and accusative plural suffixes, schematically -eh₂-es versus -eh₂-ns (subject to stem class, reconstruction and analogy). Latin still has a visible rosae : rosās distinction.

Old Irish tuatha serves both nominative and accusative plural, so this is later syncretism, not proof that PIE feminine plurals were always identical.

2. How a different plural system emerged

tuatha nom./acc. pl. two older cells converge
tuath gen. pl. reduced ending; inherited nasal grammatical environment
tuathaib prep. pl. Celtic oblique plural marker containing a -b(h)- element

The disappearance or merger of case endings may also produce root-vowel alternations, as with the cell forms mentioned in today's main lesson. These arise from earlier phonological conditions and their morphologization, not necessarily from PIE root ablaut.

3. Analogy and syncretism are innovations

Historically distinct endings can merge phonologically; speakers can then generalize a single plural pattern analogically. Identical Old Irish nominative and accusative plurals thus reflect a combination of inherited feminine structure with later Celtic and Irish reorganization.
