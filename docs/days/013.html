02 / FOCUS

The rule, clearly

Read the forms: function → change

carait ‘they love’ versus ní·carat ‘they do not love’: the plural ending changes after the negative particle; compare singular caraid : ní·cara.

The three plural person pairs are worth learning as contrasts rather than as one string of endings. carmai : ní·caram is 1st plural, carthae : ní·caraid is 2nd plural, and carait : ní·carat is 3rd plural.

The absolute 2nd plural -thae stands out. Notice that ní·caraid is 2nd plural conjunct, although the bare independent caraid is 3rd singular absolute. The particle tells you which ending series to read.

04 / HISTORICAL MORPHOLOGY

PIE → Celtic → Old Irish

Extended analysis of inheritance, sound change, analogy and innovations · optional 5–8 minutes

Historical analysis and sources (tap to collapse)

1. Person/number markers have Indo-European ancestry

The PIE finite verb distinguished first, second and third person and singular versus plural. For orientation, plural endings such as -mes (1pl), -te (2pl), and -nti (3pl) are common comparative points of departure. These are abstract comparative endings; do not mechanically derive each Old Irish W1 plural suffix from the spelling of one PIE ending.

2. How the two Old Irish series are distributed

2. How the two Old Irish series are distributed
Person W1 absolute W1 conjunct after ní
1pl carmai ní·caram
2pl carthae ní·caraid
3pl carait ní·carat

Both columns still express the same inherited person/number categories. Their surface mismatch instead reflects later Celtic inflectional history: sound changes, clitic positioning, and analogical redistribution of endings.

3. The especially revealing syncretism

The letters caraid appear as 3sg absolute but in ní·caraid they represent 2pl conjunct. The old grammar has therefore produced cross-paradigm homophony, not a mysterious shift from singular to plural meaning caused by negation. The particle + ending series jointly identifies the cell.

This makes Old Irish a useful warning for PIE reconstruction: a shared suffix's surface identity in two cells does not guarantee identity of prehistoric grammatical function.

References: Stifter (2006), §8.4; Thurneysen on absolute/conjunct flexion; Griffith (2020), “The Cowgill particle”.
