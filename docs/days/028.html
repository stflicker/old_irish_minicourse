The bare nominative–accusative plural is cride, not a regular alternate cridi ‘long plural’ form. Article and mutation context still matter.

02 / FOCUS

One point

Neuter io-stems retain the general neuter identity nominative = accusative, in singular and plural. The nominative singular cride ‘heart’ is also identical to its nominative plural in isolation, so number must be supplied by context.

To recognize singular cases, learn cride (nom./acc.), cridi (gen.), and cridiu (prepositional). The prepositional plural cridib is a good anchor for identifying plural case.

Comparing cride with the masculine céile makes the central difference clear: masculine nominative and accusative plural are different (céli/céliu), whereas neuter cride retains identity.

Source: Stifter (2006), §14.2, Illustration 14.2; UT Austin Old Irish grammatical tables, cride.

04 / COMPARISON

Historical note

Show comparison

The historical -io- stem underlies the class; in Old Irish many originally different case endings merge in their isolated appearance. The older PIE neuter nominative–accusative identity persists structurally.
