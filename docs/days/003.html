02 / FOCUS

The rule, clearly

The root vowel follows the vowel of the lost ending

In fer the root vowel changes with the case. The mutations are the same as in ech; the vowel is a second signal inside the word.

  • Before an a-coloured ending (nominative, accusative, genitive plural, prepositional plural): fer, feraib. This is a-affection, Stifter's “lowering” {L}: i > e, u > o.
  • Before *-ī, *-ū or *-e (genitive singular, nominative plural, prepositional singular, vocative singular, accusative plural): fir, fiur, firu. The root keeps its i (and o > u in son); Stifter's “raising” {R} describes the same forms from the other end.
  • The prepositional singular fiur also shows u-infection: *-ū left a u before the last consonant.

Conditions (Stifter §6.5): only stressed short vowels of monosyllables and some disyllables; never long vowels or diphthongs; raising needs a single voiced consonant after the vowel, “not all details of which have been worked out”. Which words alternate must be learned (ech and macc do not).

Quick answers

  • Is e or i the basic vowel? Stifter's chain has *i throughout, so fer is the form with a-affection. His formalism ({L}/{R}) gives the same paradigm.
  • Why does ech not give *ich? The voiceless ch blocks raising (Question 2).
  • Why f-? Initial *u̯- became f- in Irish (compare find ‘white’ < *u̯indo-, Welsh gwyn).
  • Does son work the same way? Yes: o before *a, u before *ī / *ū; a soin has oi from *-e.

Forms from Stifter p. 45 (the Primitive Irish column of the table was read in the text layer, which confuses r and i; the forms agree with the pattern of the ech table on p. 43). For Proto-Celtic *u̯iros Stifter cites the attested Gaulish spelling <uiros>.

  1. Vowel height follows the next vowel. In Primitive Irish the root vowel *i was lowered to e before *a (*u̯irah > fer) and stayed i before *ī, *ū and *e (fir, fiur, firu). This is a-affection against i- and u-affection in Irish handbooks.
  2. The endings are lost, the effect stays. After the final syllables fell, the root vowel remained as the only trace of the ending vowel, besides the mutation of the next word (vowel → L, *-ūh → H).
  3. u-infection. *-ū rounded the preceding consonant, giving fiur (and the optional euchu in ech).
  4. Replacements («). The genitive singular *-ī, the nominative plural *-oi, the prepositional plural *-obis and the vocative plural are replacements, as in ech (Day 2).
  5. Cognates. Latin vir, Sanskrit vīrá-, Gaulish uiros: the i of the root is old; the e of fer is the Irish innovation.

Summary. Regular sound change: 1–3. Analogy («): the case endings in 4.
