02 / FOCUS

The rule, clearly

Read the forms: function → change

caraid ‘(s)he loves’ becomes ní·cara ‘(s)he does not love’. The negative particle selects a different, conjunct ending.

A simple verb without a conjunct particle uses absolute endings. After ní ‘not’, it uses conjunct endings. The clearest W1 contrast is 3rd singular caraid : ní·cara, where the absolute -id corresponds to conjunct -a.

W1 (‘weak class 1’) is also called an a-verb, because its third singular conjunct ends in -a. First and second singular happen to look much alike across the two series. Keep them paired, not memorized as two unrelated verbs.

Try orally converting caraid → ní·cara and carai → ní·carai.

Source: Stifter (2006), §§8.3–8.4, W1 paradigm.

04 / HISTORICAL MORPHOLOGY

PIE → Celtic → Old Irish

Extended analysis of inheritance, sound change, analogy and innovations · optional 5–8 minutes

Historical analysis and sources (tap to collapse)

1. Inherited verbal person endings, innovative verbal classes

PIE finite verbs distinguished person and number by endings. Old Irish does too, but its weak classes include derived Celtic present stems. W1 a-verbs use an ā-type stem formative, historically associated with denominative formations. The exact derivation varies by verb; it is unsafe to treat a modern W1 form as an unchanged copy of a single PIE finite form.

The Old Irish -id : -a contrast is not simply PIE primary -ti versus secondary -t, which in other Indo-European branches principally distinguishes tense/aspect or clause function differently.

3. A major Insular Celtic innovation

A prominent account derives the absolute series from verbal forms affected by an ancient second-position particle/clitic; the conjunct series developed in environments where a preverbal element changed the clitic's position and the phonological fate of the verb ending. The particle theory is influential, not fully uncontested, and its precise prehistoric form is debated. This is morphologization of earlier syntax and prosody into competing conjugational endings.

References: Stifter (2006), §§8.3–8.4; Griffith (2020), “The Cowgill particle”; “Deriving the Old Irish Clause”.
