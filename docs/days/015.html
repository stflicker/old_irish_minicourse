tuath ‘tribe’ — no masculine-style nom./acc. identity

01 / FORMS

Paradigm or text

Singular case Form Following-word effect
Nominative tuath L
Genitive tuaithe H
Prepositional tuaith L
Accusative tuaith N
Vocative a thuath L after the noun

Tuath means ‘people, tribe, petty kingdom’. The label H is Stifter's following-word aspiration notation; it is not a final h written onto tuaithe.

02 / FOCUS

One point

The feminine a-stem class introduces a new singular pattern. For tuath, nominative tuath contrasts with accusative tuaith — compare the masculine o-stem fer where both were simply fer.

The form tuaith also serves as the prepositional singular, but its phrase-level effects differ from the accusative. The genitive is tuaithe, ending in -e after a palatalized consonant.

Instead of reciting all five forms, test the two most diagnostic ones: tuath versus tuaith.

Source: Stifter (2006), §10.1, standard feminine a-stem pattern.
