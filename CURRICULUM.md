# Curriculum architecture

## Guiding sequence

**Textbook-led, concept-sized, and spiralling.** Stifter's 58 lessons are not 58 study days. Most lessons have several sections and often require several micro-lessons. The course begins at Section 6.4 and normally proceeds along the book's prerequisite order, while the four supporting strands (syntax, texts, lexicon, and historical comparison) are woven in as each becomes usable.

```text
Nominal paradigm --- Agreement --- Clause parsing --- Connected reading
      |                  |                |
Present inflection --- Verbal complex --- Relatives / pronouns
      |                  |                |
      +---- Comparative morphology --- Historical syntax
```

The first priority is **being able to parse Old Irish forms in context**. The second is explaining their histories. Historical sophistication must not outpace synchronic competence.

## The macro-units

| Unit | Stifter anchors | Main language problem | Readiness milestone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **U01** Core inflection | 6-9 | o-stems, articles, adjectives, W1 present | Identify noun case/number and basic finite forms |
| **U02** Feminine stems and weak verbs | 10-14 | a-stems, *ben*, W2 verbs, possessives, io-stems | Analyse short inflected noun phrases and simple clauses |
| **U03** Compounds and additional stems | 15-22 | Prototonic/deuterotonic forms, ia-/i-stems, prepositional conjugation, S1-S3 verbs | Recognise a simple/compound verbal contrast |
| **U04** The finite complex | 23-29 | u-stems, copula, infixed pronouns A/B, hiatus verbs, substantive verb, verbal noun | Segment a basic verbal complex and parse short prose |
| **U05** Consonantal stems and syntactic complexity | 30-39 | Consonant stems, deponents, passive, relatives, imperatives, infixed pronouns C | Explain the function and position of markers in a clause |
| **U06** Past-time systems | 40-48 | n-stems, comparison, s-/t-/reduplicated preterites, participles, possession | Recognise multiple preterite stem types in context |
| **U07** Clause linkage and augmentation | 49-52 | Imperfect, nasalising relatives, subordinate clauses, *ro*, word order | Analyse clause dependencies and aspectual markers |
| **U08** Mood and future reference | 53-56 | Subjunctives, futures, conditional, suffixed pronouns | Contrast mood/tense formations and their contexts |
| **U09** Independent reading | 57-58 + earlier readings | Legal text, poetry, metrics, accumulated passage analysis | Produce a documented analysis of short authentic texts |

**The headings summarise topics, not every section.** Source anchors are cross-checked against the 2006 book's contents; each specific daily task will cite a section number.

### Dependencies and non-linear strands

- **Nominal sequence:** masculine/neuter o-stems → article and adjective → a-stems → io-/ia-/i-/u-stems → consonant and n-stems → irregular nouns.
- **Verbal sequence:** W1/W2 presents → simple versus compound, absolute/conjunct → strong verbs → substantive verb/copula → deponents/passive → preterite → imperfect/augment → subjunctive/future/conditional.
- **Syntax sequence:** noun phrase + article → possessives/conjugated prepositions → infixed pronouns → relatives → subordination and clause-level word order.
- **Reading sequence:** invented/graded textbook example → textbook reading excerpts → morphologically annotated glosses → independently parsed prose and poetry.
- **Comparison sequence:** thematic stem morphology → Celtic stem classes → the Insular Celtic verbal complex → prehistoric past-tense and mood formations → comparative syntax.

Keep the primary Stifter progression intact when one topic is prerequisite for another. Rearrangement is permitted when a theme is self-contained, not merely to alternate categories mechanically.

## Three phases of reading independence

**R1 | Form recognition (U01-U03).** One sentence or a few phrases. Supply lemmata, count stem forms, identify ambiguity. Exercise material may be constructed; it must be explicitly labelled.

**R2 | Guided interpretation (U04-U06).** One or two authentic short sentences, or a gloss with scholarly identification. Segment the verbal complex, describe syntactic relations, then translate. Lexical notes should not substitute for parsing.

**R3 | Independent analysis (U07-U09).** Brief prose/poetry extracts with minimal initial annotation. Attempt a literal translation first; check against a reliable edition and discuss variants only when they matter.

Do not treat every item in eDIL as a Classical Old Irish attestation: the dictionary also covers Middle and later Irish. Confirm the form's date and textual witness when interpreting historical evidence.

## How comparison should be distributed

Historical comparison starts as **one optional boxed insight every three or four sessions**, with occasional full comparison days. It should gradually become more analytic:

1. **Recognition:** e.g. Old Irish and Latin nouns reflect corresponding inherited stems.
2. **Alignment:** identify an inherited suffix or case ending at a specified historical stage.
3. **Development:** outline regular changes plus analogy and syncretism.
4. **Evaluation:** contrast different scholarly reconstructions where justified.

High-value comparative questions, in roughly increasing order: thematic nom./acc. and genitives; a-stems and PIE *-eh2*; o- versus io-stem restructuring; absolute/conjunct distribution; Goidelic/Brittonic verbal history; s-preterites and reduplication; subjunctive stems.

| U05 | Identify a relative construction and a passive/deponent form | Explain two morphosyntactic cues |
| U06 | Classify several preterite formations | Annotate a short narrative sentence |
| U07 | Locate subordination and an augment in context | Diagram clause dependencies |
| U08 | Contrast subjunctive, future, and conditional | Choose a form based on a contextualised prompt |
| U09 | Independently work through a short documented Old Irish passage | Gloss, translate, cite, and flag uncertainty |

## Pace estimate

A realistic expectation is **many months, possibly around 9-12 months or more** at 10-15 minutes per day. There is **no fixed 32-week promise**: the number of micro-lessons will depend on difficulty, forms selected, and authentic-text analysis. The initial 28 topics remain as agreed. One new topic is sent each scheduled day; the completion record is separately based on learner feedback.
