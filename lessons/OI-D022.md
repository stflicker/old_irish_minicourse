## Paradigm

**Abbreviations:** nom nominative · gen genitive · prep prepositional (dative, dat, in other grammars) · acc accusative · voc vocative · sg singular · pl plural · masc masculine · fem feminine · neut neuter · 1sg, 2sg, 3sg, 1pl, 2pl, 3pl = 1st, 2nd, 3rd person sg or pl · abs absolute · conj conjunct · deut deuterotonic · prot prototonic · pres present · pret preterite · fut future · cond conditional · impf imperfect · ind indicative · subj subjunctive · impv imperative · pass passive.

### *leicid* ‘leaves, allows’ · W2a · pres ind

| Person | Abs form | Conj form after ní |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | **leiciu / leicimm** ‘I leave’ | **ní·leiciu / ní·leicimm** |
| 2sg | **leici** ‘thou leavest’ | **ní·leici** |
| 3sg | **leicid** ‘(s)he leaves’ | **ní·leici** |
| 1pl | **leicmi** ‘we leave’ | **ní·leicem** |
| 2pl | **leicthe** ‘you (all) leave’ | **ní·leicid** |
| 3pl | **leicit** ‘they leave’ | **ní·leicet** |

W2 stands for the second weak class; the characteristic vowel of the 3sg conj is -i. The root-final consonant stays palatalized in the whole paradigm (root *leic-*).

## One point

### Compare W1 and W2: the vowel of the 3sg conj

W1: *caraid* / *ní·cara*; W2: *leicid* / *ní·leici*. In W2 the 2sg and the 3sg conj are identical (*ní·leici*), as are the 2sg abs and conj. In the 1sg the ending -im(m) became the only productive one and replaced the older -u (Stifter §12.1 note 1).

**Quick answers**

- **What are W2a verbs?** The W2 verbs that are not W2b (Day 23); many are denominative from o- and a-stem nouns (*scél* → *do·scéulai*, *rím* → *rímid*).
- **Are there W2 verbs with a non-palatalized root-final consonant?** A few originally had one (*erbaid*, *radaid*, *do·lugai*), but the difference was given up early: *radaid* became *raidid*.
- **Why are 2sg abs and conj the same?** The two forms are regularly identical in W2 (§12.3 note 2 gives the likely reason for the same in W1).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development

1. W2a verbs come mostly from denominatives of o- and a-stem nouns; some continue primary verbs with the thematic suffix \*-ei̯e/o- or athematic statives in \*-eh₁i̯e/o-.
2. At least *creitid* ‘believes’ continues the collocation \*kred dʰeh₁- ‘to put one’s heart’ (Stifter §12.3 note 5).

**Summary.** The forms are Stifter’s model table; the origins are as he states them, with his reservation that the reconstruction is rough. Unresolved: which PIE formation gave which verb.
