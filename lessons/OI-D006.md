## Paradigm
| Target | Masculine **macc** ‘son’ | Neuter **cenn** ‘head’ |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative singular | **macc** | **cenn** |
| Genitive singular | **maicc** | **cinn** |
| Accusative singular | **macc** | **cenn** |
| Nominative plural | **maicc** | **cenn** (short) / **cenna** (long) |
| Accusative plural | **maccu** | **cenn** (short) / **cenna** (long) |
| Prepositional plural | **maccaib** | **cennaib** |

*Exercise forms are model forms, not claims that every row has been attested for every noun.*
## One point
Two five-second diagnostics. **(1)** If a masculine noun ends in a palatalized consonant in the nominative plural, its accusative plural can still be a different *-u* form (*maicc* versus *maccu*). **(2)** A neuter noun retains nominative–accusative identity and may have short and long plurals.

One useful variation: some masculine *o*-stems show **u-infection** inside the root, but *macc* does **not** insert such a *u* inside its stem. Its accusative plural **maccu** has a regular plural ending; that is a different phenomenon.

Try hiding the chart and recalling just the two genitive singulars.

*Source:* Stifter (2006), §§6.7–6.8 (exercise words and variation); §§6.4–6.6 for the paradigms.
