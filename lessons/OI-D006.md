## Paradigm

Following-word effects: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. They show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

### *ech* ‘horse’ · masculine o-stem

| Case | Singular form + effect | Plural form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **ech** [0] | **eich** [L] |
| Genitive | **eich** [L] | **ech** [N] |
| Dative | **euch / eoch** [L] | **echaib** [0] |
| Accusative | **ech** [N] | **echu / euchu / eochu** [H] |
| Vocative | **a eich** [L] | **a echu / a euchu / a eochu** [H] |

### *fer* ‘man’ · masculine o-stem

| Case | Singular form + effect | Plural form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **fer** [0] | **fir** [L] |
| Genitive | **fir** [L] | **fer** [N] |
| Dative | **fiur** [L] | **feraib** [0] |
| Accusative | **fer** [N] | **firu** [H] |
| Vocative | **a fir** [L] | **a firu** [H] |

### *cenn* ‘head’ · neuter o-stem

| Case | Singular form + effect | Plural form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **cenn** [N] | **cenn** [L] / **cenna** [H] |
| Genitive | **cinn** [L] | **cenn** [N] |
| Dative | **ciunn** [L] | **cennaib** [0] |
| Accusative | **cenn** [N] | **cenn** [L] / **cenna** [H] |
| Vocative | **a chenn** [N] | **a chenna** [H] |

## One point

### Identify a form in three steps

1. **Mutation of the next word:** [0] → nominative singular of a masculine; [N] → accusative singular, genitive plural, or neuter nominative; [L] → genitive singular or nominative plural; [H] → accusative or vocative plural.
2. **The form itself:** an ending (*-u*, *-aib*), a palatal final consonant (*eich*, *fir*), u-infection (*fiur*).
3. **The sentence:** subject, object, possessor, address.

| Spelling | Possible case forms | What decides |
|---|---|---|
| ech, fer | nom. sg. [0] · acc. sg. [N] · gen. pl. [N] | mutation separates nominative; the sentence separates accusative singular from genitive plural |
| eich, fir | gen. sg. [L] · nom. pl. [L] (*fir* also voc. sg.) | the sentence; *a* before the word marks the vocative |
| echu, firu | acc. pl. · voc. pl. [H] | *a* before the word marks the vocative |
| cenn | nom./acc. sg. [N] · nom./acc. pl. [L] · gen. pl. [N] | mutation: nasalized → singular or genitive plural; lenited → plural |

Stifter's phrases show the signal on the adjective: *int ech becc* (nom.), *ind eich bicc* (gen.), *in n-ech mbecc* (acc.).

**Quick answers**

- **Which words alternate at all?** Only stressed short vowels of short words, and only some of them. Stifter's note to §6.7: *corp*, *rosc*, *sorn* and *bedg* are raisable or lowerable; *ball* has u-infection; *macc* has none; the rest keep their vowel.
- **What about long vowels?** *dál*, *síl*, *slóg*, *múr*: no change, only palatalization (§6.8.1).
- **Is *é* regular?** No: *é* turns into a diphthong before palatal consonants or with u-infection (*én* ‘bird’, nom. pl. *éoin*, dat. sg. *éun*; §6.8.1).
- **Self-test (Stifter §6.7):** decline *macc*, *dún* (n.), *corp*, *ball*, *roth*, *rosc* (n.), *sorn*, *bech*, *bedg*, *leth* (n.). Stifter gives no key; use the rules of Days 1–5.

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: why is a longer word not a copy of *fer*?

| Word | Form | Old Irish | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| *claideb* ‘sword’ | nom. sg. | **claideb** | non-palatal final *b* |
| | gen. sg. | **claidib** | palatalized *b* (\*-ī) |
| | dat. sg. | **claidiub** | u-infection (\*-ū) |
| | acc. pl. | **claidbiu** (not \*claidebu) | syncope of *e*; cluster palatalized |
| *carpat* ‘chariot’ | acc. pl. | **cairptiu** | same |
| *salann* ‘salt’ | nom. sg. · gen. sg. · dat. sg. | **salann** · **salainn** · **salunn** | spelling of the schwa follows the consonant |
| *cenel* ‘race’ | dat. pl. | **cenelaib** (no syncope) | long vowel in the second syllable (< Proto-Celtic \*kenetlom) |

Examples from Stifter §§6.8.4–6.8.6; he lists no other forms of these words.

1. **Syncope.** “In pre-Old Irish times, after the loss of inherited final syllables, the vowel of every second, non-final syllable was deleted.” A three-syllable form such as \*claidebu loses the *e*.
2. **Quality of the cluster.** If the deleted vowel was historically front (e, ē, i, ī), the whole cluster is palatalized; if back (a, ā, o, ō, u, ū), non-palatalized. In *claideb* the lost vowel was “most probably \*ī”.
3. **Counter-rules.** A long vowel in the second syllable, especially from compensatory lengthening, blocks syncope (*cenelaib*). Many analogical adjustments mean the rule often does not apply.
4. **Spelling.** The schwa is written *a* before a non-palatal and *i* before a palatal consonant: *claideb*, *claidib*; *salann*, *salainn*, *salunn*.

**Summary.** Regular sound change, with many exceptions. Stifter: “the one really has to know in each case which rules apply to a particular word”.

*Sources:* Stifter (2006) §§6.7–6.8, pp. 47–49. The relative chronology (apocope, then syncope, around the 6th century) is summarized in Wikipedia, “Primitive Irish” (secondary; McCone 1996 is the primary study).
