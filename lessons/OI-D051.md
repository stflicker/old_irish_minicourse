## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

### *guth* ‘voice’ · masc u-stem

| Case | Sg form + effect | Pl form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nom | **guth** [0] | **gothae**, **gothai**, **gotha** [H] |
| Gen | **gotho**, **gotha** [H] | **gothae** [N] |
| Prep | **guth** [L] | **gothaib** [0] |
| Acc | **guth** [N] | **guthu** [H] |
| Voc | **a guth** [L] | **a guthu** [H] |

## One point

### Mostly masc, root consonant not pal

The u-stems are the last of the ‘vocalic’ classes. They are mostly masc, a few neut; fem of this old class have all gone over to the a-stems. The root-final consonant is npal throughout, except where syncope palatalizes it secondarily: the gen of *ammus* ‘attempt’ is *aimseo*, *aimsea* (from older \*ammessu-). Some words show the *u* in the prep sg only (*biuth* ‘world’; *fis* / *fius* ‘knowledge’). The masc nom pl has three endings, the oldest being *-(a)e*; *-(a)i* was taken over from the i-stems and *-a* is younger. Neut u-stems nasalize in the nom, acc and voc sg and have a short and a long nom pl, as the neut o-stems.

**Quick answers**

- **How do I recognize a u-stem?** If the word has a root vowel *u*, or *u*-infection before its final consonant (*guth*, *giun* ‘mouth’, *ammus*). Otherwise you must know it (*bith*, *dán*, *cath*, *molad*).
- **What is the gen ending?** *-o*, later *-a*, as in the i-stems (Day 43).
- **What does ‘vocalic’ mean here?** Stem classes whose stem ends in a vowel (a, o, i, u, and combinations).
