## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

## One point

### Mostly masculine, root consonant not palatalized

The u-stems are the last of the ‘vocalic’ classes. They are mostly masculine, a few neuter; feminines of this old class have all gone over to the a-stems. The root-final consonant is non-palatalized throughout, except where syncope palatalizes it secondarily: the genitive of *ammus* ‘attempt’ is *aimseo*, *aimsea* (from older \*ammessu-). Some words show the *u* in the prepositional singular only (*biuth* ‘world’; *fis* / *fius* ‘knowledge’). The masculine nominative plural has three endings, the oldest being *-(a)e*; *-(a)i* was taken over from the i-stems and *-a* is younger. Neuter u-stems nasalize in the nominative, accusative and vocative singular and have a short and a long nominative plural, as the neuter o-stems.

**Quick answers**

- **How do I recognize a u-stem?** If the word has a root vowel *u*, or *u*-infection before its final consonant (*guth*, *giun* ‘mouth’, *ammus*). Otherwise you must know it (*bith*, *dán*, *cath*, *molad*).
- **What is the genitive ending?** *-o*, later *-a*, as in the i-stems (Day 43).
- **What does ‘vocalic’ mean here?** Stem classes whose stem ends in a vowel (a, o, i, u, and combinations).
