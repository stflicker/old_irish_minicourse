## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

### *cenn* ‘head’ · neut o-stem

| Case | Sg form + effect | Pl form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nom | **cenn** [N] | **cenn** [L] / **cenna** [H] |
| Gen | **cinn** [L] | **cenn** [N] |
| Prep | **ciunn** [L] | **cennaib** [0] |
| Acc | **cenn** [N] | **cenn** [L] / **cenna** [H] |
| Voc | **a chenn** [N] | **a chenna** [H] |

Nom = acc in both numbers (in the sg the voc too). Stifter's examples: **secht scél** ‘seven stories’ and **inna scél** ‘the stories’ (short pl); **scéla laí bráth** ‘tidings of Doomsday’ (long pl).

## One point

### Two pl: the short one is inherited, the long one was added

- **Short pl *cenn* [L].** Written like the sg; the next word is lenited, not nasalized. Used mainly after a numeral or the article, which already show the pl
- **Long pl *cenna* [H].** An extra syllable that marks the pl by itself. Stifter gives it as the only voc pl (*a chenna*).
- **Gen pl *cenn* [N]** has the same ending as the sg, as in *ech*.

**Quick answers**

- **Is [H] after *cenna* certain?** It is Stifter's default. EIEOL (§6.1) says neut pl lenite “inconsistently after those ending in *-a*”, so lenition after *cenna* also occurs.
- **Why is the voc pl *a chenna*?** The voc copies the nom/acc form, here the long one («).
- **Is *cenna* related to Latin *-a*?** The short pl is (Latin *bella*, Greek *zygá* continue PIE \*-eh₂); the long *-a* is Irish, copied from the a-stems.

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: where do the two pl come from?

| Case | Pre-Celtic (≈ PIE) | Primitive Irish | Old Irish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nom | \*kʷenneh₂ | \*kʷenā | **cenn** [L] · **cenna** [H] « |
| Gen | \*kʷennōm | \*kʷenan | **cenn** [N] |
| Prep | \*kʷennōis, \*-obʰos | \*kʷenaβih « | **cennaib** [0] |
| Acc | \*kʷenneh₂ | \*kʷenā | **cenn** [L] · **cenna** [H] « |
| Voc | \*kʷenneh₂ | \*kʷenā | **a chenna** [H] « |

Forms from Stifter p. 46. « = replaced by analogy.

1. **Short pl (regular).** PIE neut pl \*-eh₂ > Celtic \*-ā; the final vowel was lost, leaving [L].
2. **Long pl («).** The a-stem pl \*-ās (> -a, [H]) was copied onto neut nouns. Compare *tuatha* [H] (Day 15), whose \*-ās is a long vowel + \*-s.
3. **Gen pl (regular).** \*-ōm > \*-om > \*-an: same as the sg, [N].
4. **Prep pl («).** \*-obis replaced \*-ōis, as in the masc.

**Summary.** Regular sound change: 1, 3. Analogy: the long pl (2), the prep pl (4) and the voc pl.

*Sources:* Stifter (2006) §6.6, p. 46 (“the short pl … etymologically continues the Celtic and Indo-European preforms”); [EIEOL Grammar §6.1, Lenition](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/eieol/iriol/20#grammar_1276). Latin and Greek pl: standard grammars.
