## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

### *cenn* ‘head’ · neuter o-stem

| Case | Singular form + effect | Plural form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **cenn** [N] | **cenn** [L] / **cenna** [H] |
| Genitive | **cinn** [L] | **cenn** [N] |
| Prepositional | **ciunn** [L] | **cennaib** [0] |
| Accusative | **cenn** [N] | **cenn** [L] / **cenna** [H] |
| Vocative | **a chenn** [N] | **a chenna** [H] |

Nominative = accusative in both numbers (in the singular the vocative too). Stifter's examples: **secht scél** ‘seven stories’ and **inna scél** ‘the stories’ (short plural); **scéla laí bráth** ‘tidings of Doomsday’ (long plural).

## One point

### Two plurals: the short one is inherited, the long one was added

- **Short plural *cenn* [L].** Written like the singular; the next word is lenited, not nasalized. Used mainly after a numeral or the article, which already show the plural.
- **Long plural *cenna* [H].** An extra syllable that marks the plural by itself. Stifter gives it as the only vocative plural (*a chenna*).
- **Genitive plural *cenn* [N]** has the same ending as the singular, as in *ech*.

**Quick answers**

- **Is [H] after *cenna* certain?** It is Stifter's default. EIEOL (§6.1) says neuter plurals lenite “inconsistently after those ending in *-a*”, so lenition after *cenna* also occurs.
- **Why is the vocative plural *a chenna*?** The vocative copies the nominative/accusative form, here the long one («).
- **Is *cenna* related to Latin *-a*?** The short plural is (Latin *bella*, Greek *zygá* continue PIE \*-eh₂); the long *-a* is Irish, copied from the a-stems.

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: where do the two plurals come from?

| Case | Pre-Celtic (≈ PIE) | Primitive Irish | Old Irish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | \*kʷenneh₂ | \*kʷenā | **cenn** [L] · **cenna** [H] « |
| Genitive | \*kʷennōm | \*kʷenan | **cenn** [N] |
| Prepositional | \*kʷennōis, \*-obʰos | \*kʷenaβih « | **cennaib** [0] |
| Accusative | \*kʷenneh₂ | \*kʷenā | **cenn** [L] · **cenna** [H] « |
| Vocative | \*kʷenneh₂ | \*kʷenā | **a chenna** [H] « |

Forms from Stifter p. 46. « = replaced by analogy.

1. **Short plural (regular).** PIE neuter plural \*-eh₂ > Celtic \*-ā; the final vowel was lost, leaving [L].
2. **Long plural («).** The a-stem plural \*-ās (> -a, [H]) was copied onto neuter nouns. Compare *tuatha* [H] (Day 15), whose \*-ās is a long vowel + \*-s.
3. **Genitive plural (regular).** \*-ōm > \*-om > \*-an: same as the singular, [N].
4. **Prepositional plural («).** \*-obis replaced \*-ōis, as in the masculine.

**Summary.** Regular sound change: 1, 3. Analogy: the long plural (2), the prepositional plural (4) and the vocative plural.
