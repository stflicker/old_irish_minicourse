| 2nd sg. | **-t** [L] | thee |
| 3rd sg. masc. | **-a** [N] | him |
| 3rd sg. fem. | **-s** [N] | her |
| 3rd sg. neut. | **-a** [L] | it |

The 3rd singular feminine and 3rd plural *-s* may or may not nasalize in classical Old Irish (historically the feminine nasalized and the plural aspirated).

## One point

### The object goes into the middle of the verb

An **infixed pronoun** is a pronoun placed inside the verbal form, after its first particle; it is the object of the verb (the ending is the subject, and the two are independent). Old Irish has several sets; **class A** is used after preverbs and particles that historically ended in a vowel: *ro·*, *do·* (two sources), *fo·*, *ar·*, *imm·*, and the particles *no·* and *ní·*. The rules:

1. The pronoun comes after the **first** particle: in a deuterotonic compound after the first preverb (*fom·gaib*); in a prototonic compound or a conjunct simple verb right after the conjunct particle (*nim·fagaib*).
2. A simple verb in independent position has no particle, so the empty particle *no·* is added and the verb changes to its conjunct form: *caraid* ‘loves’ → *nos·cara* ‘he loves her’. With *ní·* already present, nothing is added: *ní·cara* ‘she does not love’, *nit·chara* ‘she does not love thee’.
3. The pronoun is added to the final vowel of the particle: *do·* + *-b* → *dob·*. *Ar·* and *imm·* add a vowel first (*arum·*, *immun·*).
4. The 3rd singular *-a* replaces a final *-o* (*do·* + *-a* → *da·*), and after *ní* it disappears; only its mutation remains.
5. The effects are in the table: first and second person singular lenite; the others as listed. Do not mix them up with the effects of the possessives (Day 25).

The same pronouns can refer back to the subject (no reflexive pronoun, Day 42): *na·mmarba* is ‘he kills him’ and ‘he kills himself’.

**Quick answers**

- **Are infixed pronouns exotic?** No: French *je t’aime* and Spanish *dígamelo* work the same way (an enclitic pronoun attached to the verb).
- **Where do I find all the sets?** In Stifter’s Appendix F.4.
