## Paradigm

### The infixed pronouns, class A

| Person | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | **-m** [L] | me |
| 2sg | **-t** [L] | thee |
| 3sg masc | **-a** [N] | him |
| 3sg fem | **-s** [N] | her |
| 3sg neut | **-a** [L] | it |
| 1pl | **-n**, **-nn** [0] | us |
| 2pl | **-b** [0] | you |
| 3pl | **-s** [N] | them |

The 3sg fem and 3pl *-s* may or may not nasalize in classical Old Irish (historically the fem nasalized and the pl aspirated).

## One point

### The object goes into the middle of the verb

An **infixed pronoun** is a pronoun placed inside the verbal form, after its first particle; it is the object of the verb (the ending is the subject, and the two are independent). Old Irish has several sets; **class A** is used after preverbs and particles that historically ended in a vowel: *ro·*, *do·* (two sources), *fo·*, *ar·*, *imm·*, and the particles *no·* and *ní·*. The rules:

1. The pronoun comes after the **first** particle: in a deut compound after the first preverb (*fom·gaib*); in a prot compound or a conj simple verb right after the conj particle (*nim·fagaib*).
2. A simple verb in independent position has no particle, so the empty particle *no·* is added and the verb changes to its conj form: *caraid* ‘loves’ → *nos·cara* ‘he loves her’. With *ní·* already present, nothing is added: *ní·cara* ‘she does not love’, *nit·chara* ‘she does not love thee’.
3. The pronoun is added to the final vowel of the particle: *do·* + *-b* → *dob·*. *Ar·* and *imm·* add a vowel first (*arum·*, *immun·*).
4. The 3sg *-a* replaces a final *-o* (*do·* + *-a* → *da·*), and after *ní* it disappears; only its mutation remains.
5. The effects are in the table: first and second person sg lenite; the others as listed. Do not mix them up with the effects of the poss (Day 25).

The same pronouns can refer back to the subject (no reflexive pronoun, Day 42): *na·mmarba* is ‘he kills him’ and ‘he kills himself’.

**Quick answers**

- **Are infixed pronouns exotic?** No: French *je t’aime* and Spanish *dígamelo* work the same way (an enclitic pronoun attached to the verb).
- **Where do I find all the sets?** In Stifter’s Appendix F.4.
