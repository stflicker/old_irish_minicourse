## Paradigm
| Person | Independent (absolute) | After **ní** ‘not’ (conjunct) |
|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | **caraim(m)** | **ní·caraim(m)** |
| 2nd sg. | **carai** | **ní·carai** |
| 3rd sg. | **caraid** | **ní·cara** |

The citation form **caraid** is the independent third singular present ‘(s)he loves’. A middot marks the separation after the preverbal particle.
## One point
A simple verb without a conjunct particle uses **absolute** endings. After **ní** ‘not’, it uses **conjunct** endings. The clearest W1 contrast is 3rd singular **caraid : ní·cara**, where the absolute **-id** corresponds to conjunct **-a**.

W1 (‘weak class 1’) is also called an **a-verb**, because its third singular conjunct ends in *-a*. First and second singular happen to look much alike across the two series. Keep them paired, not memorized as two unrelated verbs.

Try orally converting *caraid* → *ní·cara* and *carai* → *ní·carai*.

*Source:* Stifter (2006), §§8.3–8.4, W1 paradigm.
## Comparison (optional)
Latin often marks subject person on the verb too (*amō* ‘I love’); Old Irish, however, has the additional **absolute/conjunct selection by syntactic environment**, an Insular Celtic characteristic. The historical origin is not simply the PIE primary-versus-secondary tense distinction.
