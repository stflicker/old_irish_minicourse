## Paradigm

### Present indicative, 3rd singular: one model verb per class

| Class | Verb | Absolute form | Conjunct form after ní |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | *caraid* ‘loves’ | **caraid** | **ní·cara** |
| W2 | *leicid* ‘leaves, allows’ | **leicid** | **ní·leici** |
| S1 | *beirid* ‘carries’ | **beirid** | **ní·beir** |
| S2 | *gaibid* ‘takes’ | **gaibid** | **ní·gaib** |
| S3 | *crenaid* ‘buys’ | **crenaid** | **ní·cren** |

Stifter's model verbs (§§8.4, 12.1, 18.3, 21.4, 21.5). The citation form of a verb is its independent (absolute) 3rd singular present, because Old Irish has no infinitive.

## One point

### Three questions place every verb form

**1. Which class?** The 3rd singular conjunct decides: a final vowel means a *weak* verb (W1 *-a*, W2 *-i*); a final consonant means a *strong* verb (S1–S3). Hiatus verbs (H1–H3, *raid*, *biid*) have a vowel-final stem. The classes also differ in the palatalization of the root-final consonant:

| Class | Root-final consonant |
|---|---|
| W1 | non-palatal throughout |
| W2 | palatal throughout |
| S1 | alternates (non-palatal in 1st sg., 1st pl., 3rd pl.; palatal in 2nd sg., 3rd sg., 2nd pl.) |
| S2 | palatal throughout |
| S3 | non-palatal throughout (the *n* disappears outside the present stem) |

**2. Which series?** Every verb has two sets of endings. A simple verb with nothing before it is *independent* and takes *absolute* endings; any particle (*ní*, *in*, …) or preverb in front makes it *dependent*, with *conjunct* endings. Compound verbs have only conjunct endings; independent and dependent are then told apart by the stress (Days 31–32 in the draft).

**3. Which tense, mood, voice, person?** The person is in the ending; no subject pronoun is used (Stifter §8.3).

**Quick answers**

- **Why do I meet three classification systems?** Stifter follows McCone (W, S, H); Thurneysen's *GOI* uses A and B classes. Stifter's Appendix F.6 gives a conversion table.
- **Why two series of endings?** Not the PIE primary and secondary endings (Stifter §12.3, note 6); see Day 24.
- **Does every class have all tenses?** No. Each tense has its own stem formations, which depend on the class (preterite, subjunctive and future: Stifter Appendices F.8–F.10).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: where do the present classes come from?

| Class | Source (Stifter) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | mostly denominative (made from nouns and adjectives); many Latin loans | *marb* ‘dead’ → *marbaid* ‘kills’; *scríbaid* < Latin *scrībere* |
| W2a | mostly denominative, from o- and ā-stem nouns | *scél* ‘story’ → *do·scéulai*; *rím* → *rímid* |
| W2b | old causative and iterative verbs, root vowel \*o | *reithid* ‘runs’ → *roithid* ‘makes run’ |
| S1 | old thematic \*-e/o-: \*bʰereti, \*bʰeronti > Proto-Celtic \*bereti, \*beronti | **beirid**, **berait** |
| S2 | old thematic \*-i̯e/o-: \*gʷʰedʰi̯eti, \*gʷʰedʰi̯onti > Proto-Celtic \*gʷedi̯eti, \*gʷedi̯onti (Gaulish *uediiumi* ‘I pray’) | **guidid**, **guidit** |
| S3 | old nasal-infix presents of roots ending in a laryngeal; Proto-Celtic \*kʷrinati, \*kʷrinanti (Gaulish *prinas*, Old Welsh *prinit*) | **crenaid**, **crenait** |

1. **Weak classes are younger.** W1 and W2 are mainly built inside Irish or Celtic from nouns and adjectives; the strong classes continue inherited PIE present types.
2. **The palatalization pattern has a cause.** In S1 the thematic vowel was \*o where the root consonant is non-palatal and \*e where it is palatal (Stifter, §18.3, note 3).
3. **Absolute and conjunct** are a Celtic development, not a PIE contrast (Day 24).

**Summary.** The classification and the sources are Stifter's. Stifter states that his reconstructions reflect his own opinion; for the S2 and S3 prehistory he calls the intermediate Insular Celtic stages “far from being undisputed” (§21.6). Unresolved: the PIE input of S3 and of the hiatus verbs (not read in detail here).
