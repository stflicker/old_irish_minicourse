## Paradigm

### 1. One verb form: its parts

Reading order: first particle or preverb · (infixed pronoun) · root and stem vowel + ending. The stress falls right after the raised dot, which is the course’s way of marking the boundary (some books write a hyphen or a colon).

| Part | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| First particle or preverb | Puts the verb in dependent position; carries the meaning of a preverb or a particle such as *ní* ‘not’, *in* ‘?’ | **ní·**cara ‘does not love’; **do·**biur ‘I give’ |
| Infixed pronoun | A pronoun inside the verb: the object (‘him, her, them’) | Lessons 25, 28, 38 |
| Augment (*ro* and others) | Makes the verb perfective (‘has said’) or potential (‘can say’) | as·bert ‘said’ → as·**ru**bart ‘has said’ |
| Root and stem vowel | The meaning of the verb and its class | ní·**car-a** |
| Ending | Person, number, active or depo or pass, and the series | ní·cara**id** (2pl) |

There are no subject pronouns: the ending alone gives the person (§8.3). A verb has no infinitive; the dictionary form is the independent 3sg pres (§8.2).

### 2. Person and number, with the two series

The pres ind of *caraid* ‘loves’ (W1). Abs endings are used with nothing before the verb; conj endings after any particle or preverb (here *ní*).

| Person | Abs form | Conj form after ní |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | **caraimm** ‘I love’ | **ní·caraimm** |
| 2sg | **carai** ‘thou lovest’ | **ní·carai** |
| 3sg | **caraid** ‘(s)he loves’ | **ní·cara** |
| 1pl | **carmai** ‘we love’ | **ní·caram** |
| 2pl | **carthae** ‘you (all) love’ | **ní·caraid** |
| 3pl | **carait** ‘they love’ | **ní·carat** |

### 3. Tenses and moods

| Category | Meaning | Built on | Endings | Taught in (Stifter) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pres ind | present time | pres stem | abs and conj | L. 8, 12, 18, 21 |
| Impf | repeated or customary past action | pres stem | secondary | L. 49 |
| Pret | one completed action in the past | own stem: s-, t-, suffixless, reduplicated, long-vowel, a- or irregular | own | L. 41, 44 |
| Fut | actions to come | own stem: f-, s-, i-, a- or suppletive fut | own | L. 55 |
| Cond | would, could (hypothetical or unreal); a later action seen from the past | fut stem | secondary | L. 56 |
| Pres subj | willed, wished, uncertain, or purpose (‘so that’) | own stem: a-, e- or s-subj | own | L. 53 |
| Past subj | the same, set in the past; unreal conditions | subj stem | secondary | L. 53.6 |
| Impv | commands, to all six persons | pres stem | 2sg is the bare stem; 3sg has its own ending; the other persons equal the conj pres | L. 37 |

The secondary endings belong to exactly three categories: impf, cond, past subj. They have no abs forms; a simple verb in independent position takes the empty particle *no·*. The impv has no abs forms either.

### 4. Which form fits which time

| Time of the main clause | Same time in the subordinate clause | Later action | Subj |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present | pres | fut | pres subj |
| Past | past tenses (pret, impf, or augmented forms) | cond | past subj |

From Stifter §56.5 (the “sequence of tenses”). It is a tendency, not a strict rule as in Latin.

### 5. Voice, and forms outside the tense system

| Category | What it is | Taught in (Stifter) |
|---|---|---|
| Active | the ordinary endings | throughout |
| Depo | a lexical group of verbs with an extra *r* in the ending, active in meaning; mostly W2 | L. 31 |
| Pass | a special ending on each stem (3rd person; 1st and 2nd person); the pret pass has separate stems | L. 31, 47 |
| Rel forms | special verb forms in relative clauses (there is no relative pronoun) | L. 34, 49 |
| Vn | an abstract noun that does the work of an infinitive; formed unpredictably | L. 28 |
| Past pass participle | an adjective such as *carthae* ‘loved’, *leicthe* ‘left’ | L. 47 |
| Substantive verb, copula | *at·tá* ‘is (exists)’, *is* ‘is (identifies)’ | L. 24, 28 |

### 6. The verb classes

Every tense is built on a stem, and the stem depends on the class (§8.2, McCone’s classification). Decide by the 3sg conj.

| Class | Model verb | Abs | Conj after ní | Short description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | *caraid* ‘loves’ | **caraid** | **ní·cara** | ends in -a; root-final consonant not pal |
| W2 | *leicid* ‘leaves, allows’ | **leicid** | **ní·leici** | ends in -i; root-final consonant pal |
| S1 | *beirid* ‘carries’ | **beirid** | **ní·beir** | ends in a consonant; palatalization alternates |
| S2 | *gaibid* ‘takes’ | **gaibid** | **ní·gaib** | ends in a consonant; root-final consonant pal |
| S3 | *crenaid* ‘buys’ | **crenaid** | **ní·cren** | ends in a consonant; the *n* is only in the pres stem |
| H1–H3 | hiatus verbs | | | the root ends in a vowel |

Palatalized (pal) means pronounced with the tongue raised towards the hard palate; older grammars call it “slender”. Non-palatalized (npal) is “broad”. W stands for weak, S for strong, H for hiatus.

## One point

### Three questions place every verb form

**1. Which class?** The 3sg conj decides: a final vowel means a weak verb (W1 *-a*, W2 *-i*); a final consonant means a strong verb (S1–S3). Hiatus verbs have a vowel at the end of the root.

**2. Which series?** A simple verb with nothing before it is independent and takes abs endings. Any particle or preverb in front makes it dependent, with conj endings. Compound verbs have only conj endings; independent and dependent are then told apart by the stress (Days 31–32).

**3. Which tense, mood and voice?** Read the stem and the ending together. The three categories with secondary endings are the impf, cond and past subj; all the others have their own endings.

**Quick answers**

- **Why do I meet three classification systems?** Stifter follows McCone (W, S, H); Thurneysen’s *Grammar of Old Irish* uses A and B classes; Strachan has a third. Stifter’s Appendix F.6 gives a conversion table.
- **Why two series of endings?** They are not the PIE primary and secondary endings (Stifter §12.3, note 6); see Day 24.
- **Does every class have every tense?** Yes in principle, but each tense has its own stem formations, which depend on the class (Appendix F.8–F.10).
- **What is “secondary”?** Stifter’s name for the endings of the impf, cond and past subj (§49.1).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: where do the present classes come from?

| Class | Source (Stifter) | Example |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | mostly denom (made from nouns and adjectives); many Latin loans | *marb* ‘dead’ → *marbaid* ‘kills’; *scríbaid* < Latin *scrībere* |
| W2a | mostly denom, from o- and ā-stem nouns | *scél* ‘story’ → *do·scéulai*; *rím* → *rímid* |
| W2b | old caus and iter verbs, root vowel \*o | *reithid* ‘runs’ → *roithid* ‘makes run’ |
| S1 | old thematic \*-e/o-: \*bʰereti, \*bʰeronti > Proto-Celtic \*bereti, \*beronti | **beirid**, **berait** |
| S2 | old thematic \*-i̯e/o-: \*gʷʰedʰi̯eti, \*gʷʰedʰi̯onti > Proto-Celtic \*gʷedi̯eti, \*gʷedi̯onti (Gaulish *uediiumi* ‘I pray’) | **guidid**, **guidit** |
| S3 | old nasal-infix presents of roots ending in a laryngeal; Proto-Celtic \*kʷrinati, \*kʷrinanti (Gaulish *prinas*, Old Welsh *prinit*) | **crenaid**, **crenait** |

1. **Weak classes are younger.** W1 and W2 are mainly built inside Irish or Celtic from nouns and adjectives; the strong classes continue inherited PIE present types.
2. **The palatalization pattern has a cause.** In S1 the thematic vowel was \*o where the root consonant is npal and \*e where it is pal (Stifter, §18.3, note 3).
3. **Abs and conj** are a Celtic development, not a PIE contrast (Day 24).

**Summary.** The classification and the sources are Stifter’s. Stifter states that his reconstructions reflect his own opinion; for the S2 and S3 prehistory he calls the intermediate Insular Celtic stages “far from being undisputed” (§21.6).
