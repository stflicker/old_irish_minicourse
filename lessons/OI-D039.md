## One point

### The first three prepositions that take the prep

*Do*, *di* and *ó* all take the prep case when used alone, and so their 3rd persons use the endings -i (fem) and -ib (pl). *Do* is the only preposition that certainly has a lenited -m in the 1sg (\*dom with soft m); the others are written with plain -m. The form *dau* ‘for himself’ in one text is a hypercorrect spelling: the diphthong *au* had already become *ó* when it was written.

**Quick answers**

- **How do I tell *dó* ‘to him’ from *dó* with other meanings?** By context; the long vowel is not always marked.
- **Why do *di* and *de* both mean ‘from’?** *De* is masc/neut 3sg; *di* is fem 3sg as well as the simple preposition.
- **Does ó also mean ‘since’?** Yes.
