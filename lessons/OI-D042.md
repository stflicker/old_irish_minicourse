## Paradigm

### Pres ind of *téit* ‘goes’ · S1 (irregular)

| Person | Abs form | Conj form after ní |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | **tíagu** | **ní·tíag** |
| 2sg | **téigi** | **ní·teig** |
| 3sg | **téit** | **ní·tét** |
| 1pl | **tíagmai** | **ní·tíagam** |
| 2pl | **téit** | **ní·teit**, **ní·teigid** |
| 3pl | **tíagait** | **ní·tíagat** |

### *do·tét* ‘comes’, the compound of *téit*

| Person | Form | Phrase |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | **do·tíag** | **ní·taig**, **ní·taeg** |
| 2sg | **do·teig** | **ní·taig** |
| 3sg | **do·tét** | **ní·tait**, **ní·taet** |
| 1pl | **do·tíagam** | – |
| 2pl | – | – |
| 3pl | **do·tíagat** | **ní·taigat** |

*Form* = independent (deut); *Phrase* = dependent (prot), after *ní*. Forms marked – are not attested.

A longer form *laithe* (io-stem, neut) ‘day’ also exists.

## One point

### No reflexive pronouns; verbless clauses

A reflexive pronoun is a pronoun like English *-self* that refers back to the subject. Old Irish has none. Any pronominal construction (a conjugated preposition, an infixed pronoun, Day 56) can refer to the subject: *atom·riug* ‘I gird myself’ contains the infixed pronoun *-tom-* ‘me’ in the verb. Where it matters, context decides between ‘to him’ and ‘to himself’. The verb *téit* ‘goes’ is one of the most important irregular S1 verbs: the stem is *tíag-* (the *ía* becomes *éi* before a pal consonant), but the 3sg uses *tét-*. Clauses with prepositional phrases can often be verbless: *Carpat dam-sa!* is literally ‘a chariot to me’, translated ‘Give me a chariot!’.

**Quick answers**

- **What is a verbless clause?** A sentence whose verb must be supplied in translation; common with conjugated prepositions.
- **Why is *do·tét* irregular?** It is the compound of *téit* and shares its stems.
- **Where does *atom·riug* come from?** It is the title of Stifter’s illustration to the lorica passage (§19.1).
