## Paradigm

### The basic pattern of endings

| Person | Form | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | **-(u)m, -(u)mm** | ending in -m, often with u-infection before it |
| 2sg | **-(u)t** | ending in -t, often with u-infection |
| 3sg masc, neut | – | no rule; learn each form |
| 3sg fem | **-e** (preposition + acc), **-i** (preposition + prep) | the consonant before it is devoiced where possible |
| 1pl | **-(u)n(n)** | – |
| 2pl | **-ib** | – |
| 3pl | **-u** (acc), **-ib** (prep) | the consonant before -u is devoiced where possible |

### Same preposition, two cases

| Preposition | With the prep | With the acc |
|---|---|---|
| *in* ‘in, into’ | **indib** ‘in them’ | **intiu** ‘into them’ |
| *for* ‘on, onto’ | **fuiri** ‘on her’ | **forrae** ‘onto her’ |

## One point

### Old Irish has no free personal pronouns after prepositions

Old Irish has basically no personal pronouns (Day 12 and Stifter §8.3). Where English says ‘with me’, ‘through us’, ‘under them’, a preposition is **conjugated**: it takes person–number endings that look like verb endings only superficially. The term is Stifter’s and common in Irish studies. Historically the endings are the Proto-Celtic personal pronouns. **U-infection** is the change of a vowel to a *u*-colouring before a following *-m* or *-t* ending, written *-um*, *-ut*. In the 1st and 2nd persons the ending is the same for both cases; only the 3rd person differs.

**Quick answers**

- **Is there one set of endings?** No: each preposition differs slightly and must be learned individually; the table is only a pattern.
- **Which forms are listed?** Only attested forms, as in Stifter, GOI and OIPG; variant spellings are left to the dictionary (DIL).
- **Does 3sg have a gender distinction?** Yes: masc and neut share one form; the fem adds -e or -i.
