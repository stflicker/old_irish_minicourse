## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

## One point

### Four variants to watch

| Variant | Example | Rule (Stifter §10.2) |
|---|---|---|
| No palatalization after a cluster | **delb** ‘form’, gen. **delbae**; **deacht** ‘divinity’, gen. **deachtae** | words ending in a consonant cluster, and always words in -cht |
| *ía* becomes *e* | **cíall** ‘sense’, gen. **ceille**; **bríathar** ‘word’, prep. **breithir** | before a palatalized consonant |
| Syncope | **buiden** ‘troop’, acc. pl. **buidnea**; **dígal** ‘revenge’, gen. **díglae** | the vowel of every second non-final syllable is deleted; a front vowel palatalizes the cluster, a back vowel does not |
| Exempt from syncope | **ingen** ‘girl’, gen. **ingine**; **gabál** ‘taking’, gen. **gabálae**; **bendacht** ‘blessing’, gen. **bendachtae** | syncope already happened earlier, or the vowel is long, or the cluster would be unpronounceable |

**Quick answers**

- **Which plural forms need the mutation to tell them apart?** The nominative, accusative and vocative plural (*tuatha* [H]) are the same word; the sentence decides.
- **Why is the genitive plural *tuath* [N]?** The old ending \*-om (> \*-an) was a nasal, as in the masculine (Day 2).
- **Is raising common in this class?** Less than in the o-stems: only in monosyllables with *e* or *o* followed by one consonant (§10.2).
