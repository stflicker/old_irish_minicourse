## Paradigm
| Preposition | With article, singular prepositional | Plural prepositional |
|---|---|---|
| **do** ‘to, for’ | **don** / **dond** | **donaib** |
| **i** ‘in’ | **isin** / **isind** | **isnaib** |
| **fo** ‘under’ | **fon** / **fond** | **fonaib** |

The precise final consonant can vary with the next noun's initial sound. This table illustrates **preposition + article**, not the preposition with a possessive.
## One point
With an ordinary prepositional noun phrase, the definite article typically **fuses with the preposition**. Compare **do** ‘to, for’ with **don** ‘to/for the’; and **i** ‘in’ with **isin** ‘in the’.

One short diagnostic is whether a preposition favors the **s-containing** article variant. With **do**, we normally see *don*, but with **i**, *isin*. There are conditioned variants and exceptions, so don't turn this into an unrestricted *s*-rule.

In a phrase such as **dond fiur** ‘for the man’, you can segment the structure as *do + definite article + prepositional singular noun* even though it is written in only two words.

*Source:* Stifter (2006), §11.3; examples also in §7.1.
