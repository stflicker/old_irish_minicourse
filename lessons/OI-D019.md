## Paradigm

### Preposition + article · the system

| Preposition + … | Form | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| prep sg (all genders) | **-(s)in(d)** | do + in: **don**; fo + in: **fon**; i + in: **isin**; co + in: **cosin** |
| prep pl (all genders) | **-(s)naib** | as: **asnaib**; ar: **arnaib**; do: **donaib** |
| acc sg masc, fem | **-(s)in** [N] | fri: **frisin** |
| acc sg neut | **-(s)a(n)** [N] | fo: **foan**; i: **isan** |
| acc pl (all genders) | **-(s)na** [H] | iar: **farsna** |

### Which prepositions take which form

| Form | Prepositions (with the effect they cause) |
|---|---|
| With s | **a** (out of) [H], **co** (to, until) [H], **co** (with) [N], **for** (on), **fri** [H], **iar** [N], **i** (in) [N], **la** (with, by) [H], **ós** (over), **re** [N], **tar** (across), **tre** [L] |
| Without s | **ar** [L], **di** [L], **do** [L], **eter** [L], **fíad** [L], **fo** [L], **imm** [L], **ó** [L], **oc**, **sech** [L] |

(Stifter: *for* can take both forms; *oc* never has the s; *tre* lenites but takes the s-form.)

## One point

### In Old Irish the article always fuses

Unlike German (zum, im), Old Irish fuses article and preposition in every case, for prepositions with the prep and with the acc (§11.3). The only question is whether the form has an *s*: it follows the effect of the preposition. Prepositions that lenite use the forms without s; the rest use the forms with s.

**Quick answers**

- **Does the effect of the preposition stay?** Yes: the fused form keeps the mutations of the article (prep [L], acc [N]).
- **Why is *tre* an exception?** It lenites but takes the s-form.
- **Where is the survey of all forms?** In Stifter’s Appendix F.3.

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development

Stifter gives no history for the fusion; the article is an enclitic that attached to the preposition.

**Summary.** The rules are descriptive. Open: the origin of the s in the s-forms (not discussed by Stifter).

*Sources:* Stifter (2006) §§7.1, 11.3.
