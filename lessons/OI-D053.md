## Paradigm

### The numbers 1–10 and the effect each has on the next word

| Number | Form |
|---|---|
| 1 | **óen-** (compounded with the noun, lenition) |
| 2 | **dá** [L] (masc, neut), **dí** [L] (fem) |
| 3 | **trí** [H] (masc), **teoir** [H] (fem) |
| 4 | **cethair** [L] (neut), **cetheoir** [H] (fem) |
| 5 | **cóic** [L] |
| 6 | **sé** [H] |
| 7 | **secht** [N] |
| 8 | **ocht** [N] |
| 9 | **nói** [N] |
| 10 | **deich** [N] |

### Forming and using numbers

| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **óenchaera** | one sheep |
| **óenchiall** | one sense |
| **dá lae deac** | twelve days |
| **teoir aidchi deac** | thirteen nights |
| **a ocht deac** | eighteen |
| **a óen**, **a dau / a dó** | one, two (counting without a noun) |

## One point

### Number first, noun second; the number decides the number of the noun

The cardinal numeral stands before the counted word, and its form decides the number of the noun: the sg after *óen-* (also after 11, 21, …), the du after *dá*, *dí* (also 12, 22, …), the pl after 3–10 and 13–19, 23–29 etc. *Óen-* is compounded with the word it counts, causing lenition: *óenchaera* ‘one sheep’. The numbers 2, 3 and 4 are joined together with the noun and have gender forms; *trí* and *cethair* have their own inflection (table). The numbers 5–10 are not inflected, but they mutate the following word; *cóic* and *sé* nasalize in a genitival position. The ‘teens’ 11–19 add *deac* (later *dec*) after the counted word. When used alone, as a number rather than a counting word, a particle *a* [H] precedes: *a trí*, *a cethair*. Cardinal numerals cannot count people; a separate series exists (later lessons).

**Quick answers**

- **What is *deac*?** ‘-teen’, from Primitive Irish \*dekam-kom ‘with ten’.
- **Why does 2 take the du?** Old Irish has three numbers: sg, du (for two) and pl; this course teaches only sg./pl forms elsewhere.
- **How do you count people?** With a different numeral series, taught after Day 56.
