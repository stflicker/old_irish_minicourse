## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

### Article + noun + adjective · *ingen becc* ‘small girl’

| Case | Sg form | Pl form |
|---|---|---|
| Nom | **ind ingen becc** | **ina ingena becca** |
| Gen | **ina ingine bicce** | **ina n-ingen mbecc** |
| Prep | **dond ingin bicc** | **donaib ingenaib beccaib** |
| Acc | **in n-ingin mbicc** | **ina ingena becca** |
| Voc | **a ingen becc** | **a ingena becca** |

## One point

### The adjective copies gender, number and case; the noun class does not matter

The adjective *becc* is an o-, a-adjective: after the fem noun *ingen* it takes a-stem forms, even if the noun itself belonged to another class. Only the gender and case of the noun count (Stifter §11.4, note 1). The mutation passes along as usual: the nom sg noun lenites the adjective (*becc* with lenition of b is not written), the acc nasalizes it (*mbicc*).

**Quick answers**

- **Why is the root vowel e / i?** *becc* has raising: *bicc* before a pal consonant (gen sg *bicce*, prep *bicc*, acc *bicc*).
- **Do I need to inflect the noun and the adjective independently?** Yes, but the endings are the same.
- **What about nouns of other classes?** The adjective still follows gender and case alone.
