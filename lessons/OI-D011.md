## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the word itself changes.

### Article + noun + adjective · masculine *catt bán* ‘the white cat’

| Case | Singular form + effect | Plural form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **in catt bán** | **in chaitt báin** |
| Genitive | **in chaitt báin** | **inna catt mbán** |
| Prepositional | **don chatt bán** | **donaib cattaib banaib** |
| Accusative | **in catt mbán** | **inna chattu banu** |
| Vocative | **a chaitt báin** | **a chattu banu** |

The accusative plural of the adjective has a by-form in -a: *inna chatta bana*.

### Article + noun + adjective · neuter *clár dess* ‘the right board’ (singular)

| Case | Singular form + effect |
|---|---|
| Nominative | **a clár ndess** |
| Genitive | **in chláir deiss** |
| Prepositional | **don chlár dess** |
| Accusative | **a clár ndess** |
| Vocative | **a chlár dess** |

In the neuter plural the nominative and accusative adjective always has the long ending *-a* (Stifter §7.4).

## One point

### One noun phrase, three layers

In *in catt mbán* the article *in* [N] works on *catt*, and the noun *catt* [N] works on the adjective *bán*. Each form passes its own mutation to the next word. The adjective has the same endings as an o-stem noun: it agrees with the noun in gender, number and case.

**Quick answers**

- **What changed in the classical period?** After the classical Old Irish period the masculine nominative and accusative plural of the adjective also take the ending *-a* (§7.4).
- **Why does the nominative singular look unmarked?** The nominative singular noun is [0], so the adjective is not mutated: *in catt bán*.
- **Do I need to look at the vowels of the adjective?** Yes: genitive singular *báin* shows the palatalized consonant, as in the noun (*eich*, *fir*).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: why do noun and adjective agree?

| Case | Noun | Adjective |
|---|---|---|
| Genitive singular | **chaitt** (\*-ī) | **báin** (\*-ī) |
| Prepositional singular | **chatt** (\*-ū) | **bán** (\*-ū) |

1. Both noun and adjective are o-stems, so they received the same endings; this agreement in endings explains why the adjective repeats the case.
2. Forms follow the o-stem pattern (Days 1–5), only the neuter plural is restricted.

**Summary.** Regular o-stem development; the restrictions on the neuter plural and the by-form *-a* are Stifter’s descriptions. Unresolved: why the long form is the only one for adjectives.
