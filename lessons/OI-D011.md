## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the word itself changes.

**Abbreviations:** nom nominative · gen genitive · prep prepositional (dative, dat, in other grammars) · acc accusative · voc vocative · sg singular · pl plural · masc masculine · fem feminine · neut neuter · 1sg, 2sg, 3sg, 1pl, 2pl, 3pl = 1st, 2nd, 3rd person sg or pl · abs absolute · conj conjunct · deut deuterotonic · prot prototonic · pres present · pret preterite · fut future · cond conditional · impf imperfect · ind indicative · subj subjunctive · impv imperative · pass passive.

### Article + noun + adjective · masc *catt bán* ‘the white cat’

| Case | Sg form | Pl form |
|---|---|---|
| Nom | **in catt bán** | **in chaitt báin** |
| Gen | **in chaitt báin** | **inna catt mbán** |
| Prep | **don chatt bán** | **donaib cattaib banaib** |
| Acc | **in catt mbán** | **inna chattu banu** |
| Voc | **a chaitt báin** | **a chattu banu** |

The acc pl of the adjective has a by-form in -a: *inna chatta bana*.

### Article + noun + adjective · neut *clár dess* ‘the right board’ (sg)

| Case | Sg form |
|---|---|
| Nom | **a clár ndess** |
| Gen | **in chláir deiss** |
| Prep | **don chlár dess** |
| Acc | **a clár ndess** |
| Voc | **a chlár dess** |

In the neut pl the nom and acc adjective always has the long ending *-a* (Stifter §7.4).

## One point

### One noun phrase, three layers

In *in catt mbán* the article *in* [N] works on *catt*, and the noun *catt* [N] works on the adjective *bán*. Each form passes its own mutation to the next word. The adjective has the same endings as an o-stem noun: it agrees with the noun in gender, number and case.

**Quick answers**

- **What changed in the classical period?** After the classical Old Irish period the masc nom and acc pl of the adjective also take the ending *-a* (§7.4).
- **Why does the nom sg look unmarked?** The nom sg noun is [0], so the adjective is not mutated: *in catt bán*.
- **Do I need to look at the vowels of the adjective?** Yes: gen sg *báin* shows the palatalized consonant, as in the noun (*eich*, *fir*).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: why do noun and adjective agree?

| Case | Noun | Adjective |
|---|---|---|
| Gen sg | **chaitt** (\*-ī) | **báin** (\*-ī) |
| Prep sg | **chatt** (\*-ū) | **bán** (\*-ū) |

1. Both noun and adjective are o-stems, so they received the same endings; this agreement in endings explains why the adjective repeats the case.
2. Forms follow the o-stem pattern (Days 1–5), only the neut pl is restricted.

**Summary.** Regular o-stem development; the restrictions on the neut pl and the by-form *-a* are Stifter’s descriptions. Unresolved: why the long form is the only one for adjectives.
