## Paradigm
| Level / language | Feminine stem illustration | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| Proto-Indo-European | ***-eh₂*** | historical feminine stem-forming element |
| Proto-Celtic | ***-ā-*** | regular long-a outcome in this class |
| Old Irish | **tuath** ‘tribe’ | no visible final *-a* in nominative sg. |
| Latin | **rosa** ‘rose’ | Latin *-a* still visible in nominative sg. |
| Old Irish genitive sg. | **tuaithe** | case inflection remains, but not as Latin-style *-ae* |

The words **tuath** and **rosa** are **not cognate lexical items**: the table compares stem-class morphology.
## One point
The term **feminine *a*-stem** names a historical category, not a promise that every Old Irish case form will end in *-a*. In Celtic prehistory a stem-final long ***-ā-*** reflects an older sequence conventionally written PIE ***-eh₂*** (the numeral marks a reconstructed laryngeal).

Later Irish changes obscured that old thematic vowel; the **case system nevertheless survives** as vowel quality changes and endings such as *tuaithe* (genitive singular) or *tuatha* (plural).

For comparison, Latin often preserves a more transparent final vowel, as in *rosa*. Focus on structural inheritance rather than making Latin a parent language of Irish.

*Sources:* Stifter (2006), §§6.1, 10.1–10.2; Thurneysen, *A Grammar of Old Irish*, *ā*-stem nouns; standard Celtic/IE historical morphology.
## Comparison (optional)
Useful reconstruction chain for a **stem class**: PIE feminine ***-eh₂*** → Common Celtic ***-ā-*** → Old Irish class often called *a-stems*. Individual lexical preforms, case endings and analogical changes have more steps than this classroom-level mnemonic shows.
