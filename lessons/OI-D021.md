Attic Greek forms. Latin, Greek and Sanskrit forms are the standard paradigms; the Latin ablative and Sanskrit instrumental are separate cases.

## One point

### The same ending, kept differently

All four languages continue the PIE fem stem in *-eh₂*. Greek and Latin keep a long vowel in most forms. Irish lost all final vowels and kept their trace in the mutation of the next word and in the palatalization of the consonant before the ending (*tuaithe*). Like the o-stems, the Irish prep merges several PIE cases into one.

**Quick answers**

- **Is the Irish gen pl like the Greek?** Yes: both continue \*-om (Greek lengthened it, *khōrôn*; Irish shortened it to \*-om > [N]).
- **Which Irish pl forms are replacements?** The gen and prep pl (Day 16, «).
- **How does Irish differ from Latin?** Latin has an ablative; Irish has one prep case.

### Historical development: what is inherited?

| Case | Irish development | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Nom sg | \*-eh₂ > \*-ā > — [L] | regular |
| Gen sg | \*-eh₂s > \*-ās > [H] | regular |
| Prep sg | \*-eh₂ei, -eh₂i → \*-ai | replaced « |
| Acc sg | \*-eh₂m > \*-am > [N] | regular |
| Gen pl | \*-eh₂om → \*-om | replaced « |
| Prep pl | \*-eh₂bʰis → \*-abis | replaced « |

Based on Stifter’s tables on pp. 59–61.

**Summary.** The sg is mostly regular; the gen and prep pl and the prep sg are replacements. The comparison with Latin, Greek and Sanskrit is textbook comparative grammar.
