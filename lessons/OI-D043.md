## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

### *cnaim* ‘bone’ · masc i-stem · npal pl

| Case | Sg form + effect | Pl form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nom | **cnaim** [0] | **cnamai** [H] |
| Gen | **cnamo**, **cnama** [H] | **cnamae** [N] |
| Prep | **cnaim** [L] | **cnamaib** [0] |
| Acc | **cnaim** [N] | **cnamai** [H] |
| Voc | **a chnaim** [L] | **a chnamai** [H] |

### *súil* ‘eye’ · fem i-stem · pal pl

| Case | Sg form + effect | Pl form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nom | **súil** [L] | **súili** [H] |
| Gen | **súlo**, **súla** [H] | **súile** [N] |
| Prep | **súil** [L] | **súilib** [0] |
| Acc | **súil** [N] | **súili** [H] |
| Voc | **a shúil** [L] | **a shúili** [H] |

## One point

### An unmarked sg, an ending only in the gen

In the sg the nom, prep, acc and voc end in the pal stem-final consonant; only the gen adds an ending (*-o*, later *-a*) to the npal consonant. In the pl the endings *-i*, *-e*, *-ib* follow a consonant that is either always pal (*súil*) or always npal (*cnaim*, especially after *a*, *ó* and consonant clusters). Words in *-g* show a special alternation in the root vowel: *ai* before pal *g*, *e* before npal: *aig* ‘ice’, gen *ego*; *liaig* ‘doctor’, gen *lego*, nom pl *legai*.

**Quick answers**

- **Do all three genders have i-stems?** Yes: neut in Day 44.
- **Which differences matter in practice?** Palatalization of the pl consonant, and the nom effect.
- **Are there i-stem adjectives?** Yes, see Day 45.
