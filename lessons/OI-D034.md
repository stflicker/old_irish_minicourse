## Paradigm

### Four prepositions with the prepositional

| Preposition | Form | Meaning | With a possessive |
|---|---|---|---|
| *ass* | **as** [H] | out of | **asa** ‘out of his, her, its, their’ |
| *iarn* | **iarn** [N] | after | **iarna** ‘after his, her, its, their’, **iarnar** ‘after our’ |
| *oc* | **oc** [0] | at, by | – |
| *ós* | **ós** [0] | over, overhead | – |

### Three special adjectives

| Adjective | Form | Meaning | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| *uile* | **uile** | all, whole | before or after its noun: **int uile domun** / **in domun uile** ‘the whole world’ |
| *cech* | **cech**, **cach** | each | stands before its noun; never lenited; uninflected in the singular except feminine genitive **cacha**, **cecha**; the plural usually ends in **-a** |
| *aile* | **aile** | other, second | neuter nominative and accusative singular **aill** |

## One point

### Small words, special rules

Four prepositions join the list of Day 30: *as* (aspirates), *iarn* (nasalizes; the word is spelled *far*/*iarn* in the sources and becomes *iarn-* before a possessive), *oc* and *ós*. All take the prepositional. Three adjectives do not follow the ordinary agreement pattern: *uile* is placed freely before or after its noun, *cech* is an unstressed pronoun-like word that comes first and never lenites, and *aile* has the special neuter form *aill*. In the phrase *aill … aill …* it means ‘the one … the other …’ and is used for all genders.

**Quick answers**

- **What does *as* become before a possessive?** *asa* ‘out of his, her, its, their’, *asmo* ‘out of my’ (with lenition).
- **Is *cech* declined?** Almost not: only the feminine genitive singular *cacha*/*cecha*, and the plural usually adds *-a*.
- **What about *duine* ‘man’?** Its plural comes from a different stem: *doíni*, gen. *doíne*, prep. *doínib* (Stifter §16.1).
