## Paradigm

### *roithid* ‘makes run’ · W2b · present indicative (selected persons)

| Person | Absolute form | Conjunct form after ní |
|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | **roithiu / roithimm** | **ní·roithiu / ní·roithimm** |
| 2nd sg. | **roithi** | **ní·roithi** |
| 3rd sg. | **roithid** ‘makes run’ | **ní·roithi** |
| 3rd pl. | **roithit** | **ní·roithet** |

The other persons follow *leicid* (Day 22); Stifter says that *leicid* is the model for W2b as well. The root vowel is *o*.

### W2a and W2b side by side

| Class | Verb | Absolute form | Conjunct form after ní | Root vowel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W2a | *leicid* ‘leaves’ | **leicid** | **ní·leici** | e |
| W2a | *rímid* ‘counts’ | **rímid** | **ní·rími** | í |
| W2b | *roithid* ‘makes run’ | **roithid** | **ní·roithi** | o |
| W2b | *sluindid* ‘declares’ | **sluindid** | **ní·sluindi** | u |

## One point

### W2b is the “o / u” subtype of W2

In the present stem the two subtypes differ only in the root vowel: a short *o* or *u* marks W2b; everything else is W2a. The reason is historical: W2b verbs are old causatives and iteratives built on a root with \*o, raised to *u* in Old Irish (Stifter §12.1 note 4). Causatives mean ‘make somebody do an action’.

**Quick answers**

- **What is a causative?** A verb meaning ‘make someone do X’: *roithid* ‘makes run’ from *reithid* ‘runs’.
- **Why care?** Other tenses form their stems differently for W2a and W2b (Appendices F.8–F.10; later lessons).
- **Is *sluindid* typical?** Yes: the root vowel is *u*, and the root-final cluster is *-nd*.
