## Paradigm
| Class | Example | Meaning | Historical/root-vowel clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W2a** | **leicid** | ‘leaves, permits’ | not specifically short o/u |
| **W2b** | **roithid** | ‘makes run’ | short **o** root vowel |
| **W2b** | **sluindid** | ‘mentions, declares’ | short **u** root vowel |

**Shared W2 present-tense endings**

| Person | Absolute | Conjunct |
|---|---|---|
| 3rd sg. | **leicid** / **roithid** | **ní·leici** / **ní·roithi** |
| 3rd pl. | **leicit** / **roithit** | **ní·leicet** / **ní·roithet** |

The parallel forms of *roithid* illustrate the productive W2 pattern; orthographic variants occur.
## One point
Unlike a W1/W2 comparison, **W2a and W2b do not differ in their present ending sets**. Stifter's diagnostic is the **short root vowel o or u** in W2b. W2a covers the other W2 verbs.

The historical background helps explain the split: many W2b verbs come from old **causative/iterative** formations. *Roithid* means ‘makes run’ and is historically related to *reithid* ‘runs’. W2a includes many **denominative** verbs formed from nouns/adjectives.

**Takeaway:** Classifying a verb by its *conjunct -i* tells you **W2**, but not whether it is **W2a or W2b**. For that, inspect the root vowel and source paradigm.

**Source:** Stifter (2006), §12.1, esp. discussion of W2a/W2b and note 4; Thurneysen §§336–337.
## Comparison (optional)
W2b causative formation relates historically to Indo-European derived verbal stems with *o*-grade roots. Not all surface o/u necessarily identifies a productive causative in historical Old Irish.
