## Paradigm

### Present indicative of *beirid* ‘carries’ · S1

| Person | Absolute form | Conjunct form after ní |
|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | **biru** | **ní·biur** |
| 2nd sg. | **biri** | **ní·bir** |
| 3rd sg. | **beirid** | **ní·beir** |
| 1st pl. | **bermai** | **ní·beram** |
| 2nd pl. | **beirthe** | **ní·beirid** |
| 3rd pl. | **berait** | **ní·berat** |

### The subclasses of S1 (McCone)

| Subclass | Mark | Example |
|---|---|---|
| S1a | basic type | **beirid** |
| S1a with a | root vowel e changes to a before palatalized g or d | **laigid / legait** ‘lies’ |
| S1b | final dental becomes plain *t* when the root is unstressed | **reithid**; **ad·fet / ad·fiadat** ‘tells’ |
| S1c | root vowel a or o; palatalized only in the 2nd and 3rd sg. conjunct | **canaid, ·cain** ‘sings’; **orgaid, ·oirg** ‘slays’ |
| S1d | a nasal before the root-final consonant that vanishes outside the present | **dingid** ‘presses’; **tongaid, ·toing** ‘swears’ |

## One point

### A consonant at the end, and a root consonant that changes

Strong verbs, unlike weak verbs, have no vowel at the end of the 3rd singular conjunct. In S1 the root-final consonant alternates between palatalized and non-palatalized throughout the present. In the main type it is non-palatalized before the 1st sg., 1st pl. and 3rd pl. endings and palatalized before the 2nd sg., 3rd sg. and 2nd pl. endings. Besides this, raising and lowering of the root vowel (a-affection, i-/u-infection) may apply. Even in the earliest texts the 1st sg. sometimes ends in *-imm*.

**Quick answers**

- **What does S1 stand for?** McCone’s classification: S = strong, 1 = first class.
- **Is S1 related to Thurneysen’s class?** Stifter’s Appendix F.6 gives the conversion table.
- **Which verb is the model?** *beirid* ‘carries’, root *ber-*.

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: S1

| Stage | Form |
|---|---|
| PIE | \*bʰereti, \*bʰeronti |
| Proto-Celtic | \*bereti, \*beronti |
| Old Irish | **beirid**, **berait** |

S1 verbs are old thematic verbs in \*-e/o-. Where the S1a verb has non-palatalized root consonant, the thematic vowel was historically \*o; where palatalized, it was \*e.
