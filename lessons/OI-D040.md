### The subclasses of S1 (McCone)

| Subclass | Mark | Example |
|---|---|---|
| S1a | basic type | **beirid** |
| S1a with a | root vowel e changes to a before palatalized g or d | **laigid / legait** ‘lies’ |
| S1b | final dental becomes plain *t* when the root is unstressed | **reithid**; **ad·fet / ad·fiadat** ‘tells’ |
| S1c | root vowel a or o; palatalized only in the 2nd and 3sg conj | **canaid, ·cain** ‘sings’; **orgaid, ·oirg** ‘slays’ |
| S1d | a nasal before the root-final consonant that vanishes outside the present | **dingid** ‘presses’; **tongaid, ·toing** ‘swears’ |

## One point

### A consonant at the end, and a root consonant that changes

Strong verbs, unlike weak verbs, have no vowel at the end of the 3sg conj. In S1 the root-final consonant alternates between palatalized and non-palatalized throughout the present. In the main type it is non-palatalized before the 1sg, 1pl and 3pl endings and palatalized before the 2sg, 3sg and 2pl endings. Besides this, raising and lowering of the root vowel (a-affection, i-/u-infection) may apply. Even in the earliest texts the 1sg sometimes ends in *-imm*.

**Quick answers**

- **What does S1 stand for?** McCone’s classification: S = strong, 1 = first class.
- **Is S1 related to Thurneysen’s class?** Stifter’s Appendix F.6 gives the conversion table.
- **Which verb is the model?** *beirid* ‘carries’, root *ber-*.

### Historical development: S1

| Stage | Form |
|---|---|
| PIE | \*bʰereti, \*bʰeronti |
| Proto-Celtic | \*bereti, \*beronti |

S1 verbs are old thematic verbs in \*-e/o-. Where the S1a verb has non-palatalized root consonant, the thematic vowel was historically \*o; where palatalized, it was \*e.
