## One point

### The ending is always a vowel: e, i, u

Historically the io-stems were only a variant of the o-stems (an *i* before the *o*), but the sound changes made their inflection different: there is always a vowel at the end of the word (Stifter §14.1). The mutations parallel the o-stems. The mnemonic is *fer*: e as in *fer*, i as in *fir*, u as in *fiur*. The consonant before the ending is palatalized (*céile*) or non-palatalized (*daltae*) throughout the paradigm; the two sets differ only in spelling (e/i/u after a palatalized consonant, ae/ai/u after a non-palatalized one).

**Quick answers**

- **Which words are non-palatalized?** Mainly words with a consonant cluster before the final vowel (*daltae*).
- **Does the root vowel change?** No raising or lowering takes place in the root.
- **Which forms are alike?** Gen sg and nom pl (*céili* [L]); nom and acc sg differ by their mutation ([H] vs [N]).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development

Historically the io-stems are a variant of the PIE o-stems with a \*-i̯- before the thematic vowel. After the loss of final syllables the vowel of the ending (e, i, u) stayed in the word. Stifter’s mnemonic table connects each ending with the corresponding form of *fer*.

**Summary.** Regular development with the same mutations as the o-stems. Unresolved: the exact Primitive Irish stages (not readable in the text layer).
