## Paradigm
| Case | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **céile** | **céli** |
| Genitive | **céli** | **céile** |
| Prepositional | **céliu** | **céilib** |
| Accusative | **céile** | **céliu** |
| Vocative | **a chéli** | **a chéliu** |

Pay particular attention to **accusative singular céile**, not *céliu*. The latter is accusative **plural** (and prepositional singular).
## One point
Masculine **io-stems** reflect prehistoric *-io-* beside the *o*-stems already studied. In this type the nominative singular **céile** ‘companion’ has an overt final vowel, and the genitive singular **céli** differs.

The most important opposition is **céile** (nominative/accusative singular) versus **céliu** (prepositional singular/accusative plural). Syncretism reappears, but the pairings are not those of *ech*.

**Practice:** identify *céile* as possible nominative or accusative singular, then read *céliu* in context.

**Source:** Stifter (2006), §14.1; corroborated by UT Austin's Old Irish lessons, *céile* declension.

## Comparison (optional)

### 1. *io*-stems are a branch of the inherited thematic system

The historical **masculine *-io*-stem** belongs to a PIE/early Indo-European ***-i̯o-*** formation, structurally related to simple ***-o-*** stems but with an additional palatal glide ***i̯*** before the thematic vowel. Old Irish **céile** ‘companion’ is a representative masculine *io*-stem.

### 2. Why didn't these nouns develop exactly like *fer*?

| Case | Old Irish **céile** | Comparison with ordinary *o*-stem **fer** |
|---|---|---|
| Nom. sg. | **céile** | **fer** |
| Gen. sg. | **céli** | **fir** |
| Prep. sg. | **céliu** | **fiur** |
| Nom. pl. | **céli** | **fir** |
| Acc. pl. | **céliu** | **firu** |

In *io*-stems, the prehistoric **glide and adjacent vowels** affected the outcome of later Celtic/Irish sound changes. This often left an **overt final vowel (*-e, -i, -u*)** where a simple *o*-stem appears to have no ending.

### 3. Innovation and inheritance

The ***-i̯o-*** stem formation is **inherited**; the **specific Irish distribution of surviving final vowels** reflects sound change and some paradigm leveling. The **same Celtic genitive ***-ī*** innovation that affects ordinary thematic stems also matters to interpreting *io*-stems.

Avoid equating the different case-final **-e/-i/-u** directly with three PIE endings: each represents **the result of a longer phonological history**. The *io*-stem's case effects on following words mostly parallel those of masculine *o*-stems.

**References:** Stifter (2006), §14.1; [University of Texas, *céile* declension](https://lrc.la.utexas.edu/eieol/iriol/30); Thurneysen on *io*-stems.
