## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the adjective itself changes.

**Abbreviations:** nom nominative · gen genitive · prep prepositional (dative, dat, in other grammars) · acc accusative · voc vocative · sg singular · pl plural · masc masculine · fem feminine · neut neuter · 1sg, 2sg, 3sg, 1pl, 2pl, 3pl = 1st, 2nd, 3rd person sg or pl · abs absolute · conj conjunct · deut deuterotonic · prot prototonic · pres present · pret preterite · fut future · cond conditional · impf imperfect · ind indicative · subj subjunctive · impv imperative · pass passive.

## One point

### Sg like the noun, pl of its own

In the sg io-stem adjectives are identical with masc, respectively neut io-stem nouns (Days 27–28). In the pl they differ: the endings are *-(a)i* (nom, acc, voc), *-(a)e* (gen) and *-(a)ib* (prep) for all genders. The root-final consonant is palatalized (*buide*) or non-palatalized (*rígdae*) throughout. The fem counterpart is the ia-stem (Day 36).

**Quick answers**

- **How do I know an adjective is an io-stem?** If it ends in *-e* in the nom sg (Stifter §23.4, Day 52).
- **Which forms of the neut pl lenite?** The nom, acc and voc pl are [L].
- **Do they take the article and noun together?** Yes, exactly as the o-, a-adjectives (Day 11).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development

Historically the io-adjectives are the variant of o-adjectives with an \*-i̯- before the thematic vowel, just as the io-stem nouns are a variant of the o-stems.
