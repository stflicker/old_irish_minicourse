## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the adjective itself changes.

### *buide* ‘yellow’ · io-stem adjective · masculine

| Case | Singular form + effect | Plural form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **buide** [H] | **buidi** [L] |
| Genitive | **buidi** [L] | **buide** [N] |
| Prepositional | **buidiu** [L] | **buidib** [0] |
| Accusative | **buide** [N] | **buidi** [H] |
| Vocative | **a buidi** [L] | **a buidi** [H] |

## One point

### Singular like the noun, plural of its own

In the singular io-stem adjectives are identical with masculine, respectively neuter io-stem nouns (Days 27–28). In the plural they differ: the endings are *-(a)i* (nominative, accusative, vocative), *-(a)e* (genitive) and *-(a)ib* (prepositional) for all genders. The root-final consonant is palatalized (*buide*) or non-palatalized (*rígdae*) throughout. The feminine counterpart is the ia-stem (Day 36).

**Quick answers**

- **How do I know an adjective is an io-stem?** If it ends in *-e* in the nominative singular (Stifter §23.4, Day 52).
- **Which forms of the neuter plural lenite?** The nominative, accusative and vocative plural are [L].
- **Do they take the article and noun together?** Yes, exactly as the o-, a-adjectives (Day 11).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development

Historically the io-adjectives are the variant of o-adjectives with an \*-i̯- before the thematic vowel, just as the io-stem nouns are a variant of the o-stems.
