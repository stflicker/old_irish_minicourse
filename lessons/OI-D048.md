## Paradigm

### Pres ind of *gaibid* ‘takes’ · S2

| Person | Abs form | Conj form after ní |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | **gaibiu**, **gaibimm** | **ní·gaibiu**, **ní·gaibimm** |
| 2sg | **gaibi** | **ní·gaibi** |
| 3sg | **gaibid** | **ní·gaib** |
| 1pl | **gaibmi** | **ní·gaibem** |
| 2pl | **gaibthe** | **ní·gaibid** |
| 3pl | **gaibit** | **ní·gaibet** |

## One point

### Pal throughout, ends in a consonant

S2 is the second strong class: the stem-final consonant is pal in every form, so it is similar to W2 (Day 22), but the 3sg conj ends in the plain root consonant. As in W2 the 1sg *-iu* is replaced by *-imm* during the Old Irish period. Compound S2 verbs follow the usual rules (Days 31–33); syncope can depalatalize the root-final consonant: *fo·gaib* ‘finds’, prot *·fagaib*, but 3pl *·fagbat*. Some verbs show confusion with S1: 1sg *ad·gaur* ‘I sue’ (u-infection, no ending), 3pl *du·airngerat* ‘they promise’.

**Quick answers**

- **How do I tell S2 from W2?** By the 3sg conj: *ní·gaib* (S2) versus *ní·leici* (W2).
- **What are the other S2 verbs?** *guidid* ‘asks, prays’, *fo·daim* ‘suffers’, *fris·gair* ‘answers’ (Stifter exercise 21.7).
- **Does the 1sg abs differ?** Only in -iu / -imm.
