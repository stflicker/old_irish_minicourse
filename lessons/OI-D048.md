## One point

### Palatalized throughout, ends in a consonant

S2 is the second strong class: the stem-final consonant is palatalized in every form, so it is similar to W2 (Day 22), but the 3rd singular conjunct ends in the plain root consonant. As in W2 the 1st singular *-iu* is replaced by *-imm* during the Old Irish period. Compound S2 verbs follow the usual rules (Days 31–33); syncope can depalatalize the root-final consonant: *fo·gaib* ‘finds’, prototonic *·fagaib*, but 3rd plural *·fagbat*. Some verbs show confusion with S1: 1st sg. *ad·gaur* ‘I sue’ (u-infection, no ending), 3rd pl. *du·airngerat* ‘they promise’.

**Quick answers**

- **How do I tell S2 from W2?** By the 3rd sg. conjunct: *ní·gaib* (S2) versus *ní·leici* (W2).
- **What are the other S2 verbs?** *guidid* ‘asks, prays’, *fo·daim* ‘suffers’, *fris·gair* ‘answers’ (Stifter exercise 21.7).
- **Does the 1st sg. absolute differ?** Only in -iu / -imm.
