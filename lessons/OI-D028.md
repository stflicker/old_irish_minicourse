## Paradigm
| Case | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **cride** | **cride** |
| Accusative | **cride** | **cride** |
| Genitive | **cridi** | **cride** |
| Prepositional | **cridiu** | **cridib** |
| Vocative | **a chride** | **a chride** |

The **bare** nominative–accusative plural is **cride**, *not* a regular alternate *cridi* ‘long plural’ form. Article and mutation context still matter.
## One point
Neuter **io-stems** retain the general neuter identity **nominative = accusative**, in singular and plural. The nominative singular **cride** ‘heart’ is also identical to its nominative plural in isolation, so number must be supplied by context.

To recognize singular cases, learn **cride** (nom./acc.), **cridi** (gen.), and **cridiu** (prepositional). The prepositional plural **cridib** is a good anchor for identifying plural case.

Comparing **cride** with the masculine **céile** makes the central difference clear: masculine nominative and accusative plural are different (*céli/céliu*), whereas neuter *cride* retains identity.

**Source:** Stifter (2006), §14.2, Illustration 14.2; UT Austin Old Irish grammatical tables, *cride*.

## Comparison (optional)

### 1. What was inherited from PIE?

The Old Irish **neuter *io*-stem** continues an old **thematic ***-i̯o-*** type. In the PIE neuter system the **nominative and accusative were identical** in both singular and plural. For a reconstructed thematic singular, compare the **neuter ***-i̯om*** type**; the key point is the **identity of grammatical cases**, not a guaranteed direct spelling for *cride*.

### 2. Follow one noun through the paradigm

| Cell | Old Irish | Historical interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| nom./acc. sg. | **cride** | a neuter *io*-stem ending surviving after changes |
| gen. sg. | **cridi** | Celtic thematic genitive morphology |
| prep. sg. | **cridiu** | old oblique vowel environment |
| nom./acc. pl. | **cride** | retained neuter case identity and reduced old plural morphology |
| prep. pl. | **cridib** | oblique plural in a *-b(h)-* class |

The shared **cride** singular/plural nominative–accusative is **surface syncretism**; the noun phrase can still distinguish the numbers by articles or modifiers.

### 3. Innovation alongside conservatism

The **neuter case identity is conservative**, while the **visible form** underwent massive Celtic/Irish reanalysis. Unlike some neuter *o*-stems, the bare *io*-stem **cride** does **not** have a regular nom./acc. long plural ***cridi***. One must establish **class-specific analogy** from evidence, not assume that every neuter forms *-a* or *-i* plurals.

The lexical root of **cride** ‘heart’ is historically related to other IE ‘heart’ words, e.g. Latin **cor, cordis**, but they belong to **different noun-stem formations**. A lexical cognate does **not** guarantee the same inflectional class.
