## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

## One point

### Neuter io-stems parallel masculine io-stems as neuter o-stems parallel masculine o-stems

The neuter nominative and accusative are identical, and they cause the same mutations as in *cenn* ([N] singular, [L] plural). The mnemonic is *cenn*: *cenn* (e), *cinn* (i), *ciunn* (u). Again the consonant before the ending is palatalized (*cride*) or non-palatalized (*cumachtae*) throughout the paradigm, and the root vowel does not change (§14.2).

**Quick answers**

- **Is there a long plural like *cenna*?** Not in the table: the plural of the neuter io-stems has the same form as the singular.
- **How do I tell cride [N] from cride [L]?** By the mutation of the next word, since the forms are spelled alike.
- **Which forms differ by number?** The prepositional (-iu / -ib) and the genitive (-i / -e).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development

Neuter io-stems stand to the masculine io-stems as the neuter o-stems to the masculine (Day 4). The gen. and prep. forms continue the same endings as the masculine.

**Summary.** Regular development; the mutations are those of *cenn*. Unresolved: the plural accusative and genitive forms are partly illegible in the text layer and were taken from the pattern.
