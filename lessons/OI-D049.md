## One point

### The third logical possibility

S1 alternates between palatalized and non-palatalized, S2 is palatalized throughout, and S3 is non-palatalized throughout: this is the third logical possibility. The root-final consonant is always *n*, which disappears outside the present stem, so *crenaid* has the root *cren-* and the past and other stems lack the *n*. McCone has only one S3; Stifter distinguishes S3a (vowel *e* in the present, Thurneysen B IV) and S3b (vowel *i* or *u*, Thurneysen B V). S3a is by far the better attested. No simple active S3b verb is attested. The one exception to the non-palatalized *n* is the deponent *ro·cluinethar* ‘hears’ (stem *cluin-*). Secondary palatalization appears when a vowel before *n* is syncopated: *do·fuibnimm* ‘I cut’ < \*to-foben-aimm.

**Quick answers**

- **Why is S3 called strong?** The 3rd sg. conjunct ends in a consonant.
- **What is a deponent verb?** A verb with active meaning but its own endings; taught after Day 56.
- **Other S3a verbs?** *renaid* ‘sells’, *fris·ben* ‘heals’, *for·fen* ‘finishes’ (Stifter exercise 21.7).
