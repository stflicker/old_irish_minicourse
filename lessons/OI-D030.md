## Paradigm

### New prepositions

| Preposition | Form | Meaning | Case after it |
|---|---|---|---|
| *cen* | **cen** [L] | without | accusative |
| *co* | **co** [N] | with | prepositional |
| *di* | **di** [L] | from | prepositional |
| *fo* | **fo** [L] | under: ‘placed under’ with the prepositional, ‘going under’ with the accusative | prepositional or accusative |
| *fri* | **fri** [H] | towards, against | accusative |
| *ó* | **ó** [L] | from | prepositional |
| *tar* | **tar** | across | accusative |

The effect letters show what the preposition does to the next word (Days 1–5). Like all prepositions, these fuse with the article (Day 19).

### A question particle

| Particle | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *innad* | **innad** [N] | negative question ‘do … not?’ (takes the conjunct form of the verb) |

## One point

### Position or direction: the case tells which

Old Irish prepositions govern a case: the prepositional (usually place, position) or the accusative (usually direction, movement). Four prepositions can take both, with a change in meaning: *i(n)*, *for*, *ar* and *fo* (Stifter §18.1). *Fo* ‘under’ is the first example: *fo* + prepositional = at rest, *fo* + accusative = into.

**Quick answers**

- **Do these prepositions have conjugated forms?** Yes (Day 39 for *di*, *ó*, *fri*; others later), e.g. *dom* ‘to me’.
- **What is *cen* + a verbal noun?** Not treated here.
- **Does the preposition mutate the article?** The mutation is part of the fused form: *co* + article keeps [N].
