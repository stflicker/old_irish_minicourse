## Paradigm

Following-word effects: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration / gemination · [0] none. They describe the *next* word, not the noun's own ending.

### *cenn* ‘head’ · neuter o-stem · singular

| Case | Singular form + effect |
|---|---|
| Nominative | **cenn** [N] |
| Genitive | **cinn** [L] |
| Prepositional | **ciunn** [L] |
| Accusative | **cenn** [N] |
| Vocative | **a chenn** [N] |

In a neuter the nominative, accusative and vocative are one form. The vocative particle *a* lenites *c-* (*chenn*); the noun's own [N] is the effect on the *next* word.

## One point

### A neuter has one ending where a masculine has two

**Nominative = accusative = vocative: *cenn* [N].** A masculine keeps nominative [0] against accusative [N] (*ech*); a neuter does not, because its nominative *is* the old accusative.

**Quick answers**

- **Why one form for two cases?** In PIE neuters the nominative and accusative singular share \*-om (Latin *bellum*, Greek *zygón*, Sanskrit *yugám*). It is the masculine accusative ending used for the subject too.
- **Why *c-* here but *ch* in *ech*?** Initial \*kʷ gave *c*; between vowels it was lenited first (Day 1). Welsh *pen* shows the Brittonic result, *p*.
- **Why *nn*?** Stifter's Pre-Celtic form already has *nn* (\*kʷennom). I found no secure PIE etymology for ‘head’; Matasović (2009) lists it under Celtic (not checked directly).
- **Why *cinn*, *ciunn*?** \*-ī and \*-ū are high vowels, as in *fir*, *fiur* (Day 3); \*-ū also rounds (*iu*).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical card: why do *cenn*, *cinn*, *ciunn* look so different?

| Cell | Pre-Celtic (≈ PIE) | Primitive Irish | Old Irish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative / accusative | \*kʷennom | \*kʷenan | **cenn** [N] |
| Genitive | \*kʷennosi̯o | \*kʷenī « | **cinn** [L] |
| Prepositional | \*kʷennōi̯, \*-oh₁, \*-ōd | \*kʷenū | **ciunn** [L] |
| Vocative | \*kʷennom | \*kʷenan | **a chenn** [N] |

Forms from Stifter p. 46 (he gives no Proto-Celtic column here). « = replaced by analogy.

1. **Neuter ending.** \*-om > \*-an: the neuter nominative/accusative nasalizes (as the masculine accusative does, Day 1).
2. **Raising and rounding.** \*-ī and \*-ū are high vowels: *e* > *i* (*cinn*), and \*-ū also leaves *u*-infection (*ciunn*).
3. **Initial \*kʷ.** Goidelic \*kʷ > *c*; Brittonic and Gaulish \*kʷ > *p* (Welsh *pen*, Gaulish *penno-*).
4. **Genitive («).** The \*-ī replaced PIE \*-osi̯o, as in the masculine.

**Status.** Regular: 1–3. Analogical: 4. Open: the PIE source of the root.
