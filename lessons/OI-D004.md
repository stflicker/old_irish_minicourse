## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

### *cenn* ‘head’ · neut o-stem · sg

| Case | Sg form + effect |
|---|---|
| Nom | **cenn** [N] |
| Gen | **cinn** [L] |
| Prep | **ciunn** [L] |
| Acc | **cenn** [N] |
| Voc | **a chenn** [N] |

In a neut the nom, acc and voc are one form. The voc particle *a* lenites *c-* (*chenn*); the noun's own [N] is the effect on the *next* word.

## One point

### A neut has one ending where a masc has two

**Nom = acc = voc: *cenn* [N].** A masc keeps nom [0] against acc [N] (*ech*); a neut does not, because its nom *is* the old acc.

**Quick answers**

- **Why one form for three cases?** In PIE neut the nom and acc share \*-om (Latin *bellum*, Greek *zygón*, Sanskrit *yugám*): the masc acc ending used for the subject too. The voc of a neut is the nom.
- **Why *c-* here but *ch* in *ech*?** Initial \*kʷ gave *c*; between vowels it was lenited first (Day 1). Welsh *pen* shows the Brittonic result, *p*.
- **Why *nn*?** Stifter's Pre-Celtic form already has *nn* (\*kʷennom). No secure PIE etymology for ‘head’ is established; Matasović (2009) lists it under Celtic (not checked directly).
- **Why *cinn*, *ciunn*?** \*-ī and \*-ū are high vowels, as in *fir*, *fiur* (Day 3); \*-ū also adds u-infection (*iu*).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: why do *cenn*, *cinn* and *ciunn* look so different?

| Case | Pre-Celtic (≈ PIE) | Primitive Irish | Old Irish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nom | \*kʷennom | \*kʷenan | **cenn** [N] |
| Gen | \*kʷennosi̯o | \*kʷenī « | **cinn** [L] |
| Prep | \*kʷennōi̯, \*-oh₁, \*-ōd | \*kʷenū | **ciunn** [L] |
| Acc | \*kʷennom | \*kʷenan | **cenn** [N] |
| Voc | \*kʷennom | \*kʷenan | **a chenn** [N] |

Forms from Stifter p. 46 (he gives no Proto-Celtic column here). « = replaced by analogy.

1. **Neut ending.** \*-om > \*-an: the neut nom and acc nasalize, like the masc acc (Day 1).
2. **Root vowel and u-infection.** \*-ī and \*-ū are high vowels, so the root vowel is *i* (*cinn*), and \*-ū also leaves u-infection (*ciunn*), as in *fiur*.
3. **Initial \*kʷ.** Goidelic \*kʷ > *c*; Brittonic and Gaulish \*kʷ > *p* (Welsh *pen*, Gaulish *penno-*).
4. **Gen («).** The \*-ī replaced PIE \*-osi̯o, as in the masc.

**Summary.** Regular sound change: 1–3. Analogy: 4. Unresolved: the PIE source of the root.
