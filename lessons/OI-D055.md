## Paradigm

### The independent (abs) forms of the copula

| Person | Abs form |
|---|---|
| 1sg | **am** |
| 2sg | **it**, **at** |
| 3sg | **is** |
| 1pl | **ammin**, **ammi** |
| 2pl | **adib**, **adi** |
| 3pl | **it** |

### The dependent (conj) forms: added to a particle

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| 1sg | **-da** [L] |
| 2sg | **-da** [L] |
| 3sg | **-t**, **-id**, **-did** |
| 1pl | **-dan** [L] |
| 2pl | **-dad** [L] |
| 3pl | **-dat** [L] |

### With ní ‘not’ and in ‘?’

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ní** | she / he is not |
| **níta**, **nída** | I am not; you are not |
| **nítan**, **nídan** | we are not |
| **inda** | am I? art thou? |
| **indan** | are we? |
| **in** | is he / she / it? |

## One point

### The verb for equating two things

The copula links a subject with a noun or adjective: *am Duid-se* ‘I am David’, *is mór in truaige* ‘the sorrow is great’, *at ben danae* ‘thou art a bold woman’. A rule of thumb: wherever you can put ‘=’ between two words, use the copula. A connection with a prepositional phrase, a place, existence or a condition needs the **substantive verb** (taught in a later lesson; it also has a habitual variant). Spanish *ser* (copula) and *estar* (substantive verb) have a similar split. The copula is never accented: it is **enclitic** and merges with preceding particles. The usual order is copula–predicate–subject (*is fer Eoin*). Emphasis is shown by fronting a phrase after *is*, with the rest as a relative clause. After *cia* ‘although’ and *ma* ‘if’ special 3rd-person forms are used (*cia·su*, *mas*, *manid*).

**Quick answers**

- **Can I use the substantive verb instead?** Often yes, especially if the predicate does not follow the verb.
- **Why do the conj forms lenite?** Stifter says he has no explanation (except for the 3sg). I searched other sources and found none that explains it; the general account of dependent forms (a particle placed before the verb) does not cover the copula’s lenition. Treat it as a fact to learn.

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development

| Person | Old Irish | Proto-Celtic |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | **am** | \*emmi |
| 2sg | **at** | \*esi |
| 3sg | **is** | \*esti |

The forms come from the PIE root \*h₁es- ‘to be’. Stifter calls the reconstructions “highly conjectural”.
