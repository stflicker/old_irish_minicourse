## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

**Abbreviations:** nom nominative · gen genitive · prep prepositional (dative, dat, in other grammars) · acc accusative · voc vocative · sg singular · pl plural · masc masculine · fem feminine · neut neuter · 1sg, 2sg, 3sg, 1pl, 2pl, 3pl = 1st, 2nd, 3rd person sg or pl · abs absolute · conj conjunct · deut deuterotonic · prot prototonic · pres present · pret preterite · fut future · cond conditional · impf imperfect · ind indicative · subj subjunctive · impv imperative · pass passive.

### *muir* ‘sea’ · neut i-stem

| Case | Sg form + effect | Pl form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nom | **muir** [N] | **muire** [L] |
| Gen | **moro**, **mora** [H] | **muire** [N] |
| Prep | **muir** [L] | **muirib** [0] |
| Acc | **muir** [N] | **muire** [L] |
| Voc | **a muir** [N] | **a muire** [L] |

## One point

### Sg as in masc and fem, pl -e

Neut i-stems are not very frequent. In the sg they look like masc or fem i-stems; the only difference is that the nom, acc and voc nasalize the following word. In the pl the nom, acc and voc end in *-e* [L]; the gen and prep pl are *-e* [N] and *-ib* [0]. As in Day 43 the gen sg lowers the root vowel (*muir* → *moro*).

**Quick answers**

- **Is the gen pl different from the nom pl?** Only in the effect: both are *muire*, but [N] versus [L].
- **Do neut i-stem adjectives exist?** Adjectives with i-stems are treated in Day 45.
- **Is *muir* common?** Yes, it is the model word for this class (Stifter §20.2).
