## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

### *muir* ‘sea’ · neuter i-stem

| Case | Singular form + effect | Plural form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **muir** [N] | **muire** [L] |
| Genitive | **moro**, **mora** [H] | **muire** [N] |
| Prepositional | **muir** [L] | **muirib** [0] |
| Accusative | **muir** [N] | **muire** [L] |
| Vocative | **a muir** [N] | **a muire** [L] |

## One point

### Singular as in masculine and feminine, plural -e

Neuter i-stems are not very frequent. In the singular they look like masculine or feminine i-stems; the only difference is that the nominative, accusative and vocative nasalize the following word. In the plural the nominative, accusative and vocative end in *-e* [L]; the genitive and prepositional plural are *-e* [N] and *-ib* [0]. As in Day 43 the genitive singular lowers the root vowel (*muir* → *moro*).

**Quick answers**

- **Is the genitive plural different from the nominative plural?** Only in the effect: both are *muire*, but [N] versus [L].
- **Do neuter i-stem adjectives exist?** Adjectives with i-stems are treated in Day 45.
- **Is *muir* common?** Yes, it is the model word for this class (Stifter §20.2).
