## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the pronoun itself changes.

### Poss pronouns (not inflected; always before the noun)

| Person | Poss form + effect | Example (constructed by the rule) |
|---|---|---|
| my | **mo** [L] (before a vowel: **m’**) | **m’ainm** ‘my name’ |
| thy | **do** [L] (before a vowel: **t’** / **th’**) | **t’ainm** ‘thy name’ |
| his | **a** [L] | **a ainm** ‘his name’ |
| her | **a** [H] | **a hainm** ‘her name’ |
| its | **a** [L] | **a ainm** ‘its name’ |
| our | **ar** [N] | **ar n-ainm** ‘our name’ |
| your | **for / far / bar** [N] | **far n-ainm** ‘your name’ |
| their | **a** [N] | **a n-ainm** ‘their name’ |

The examples follow the stated mutations; Stifter’s own examples are *m’athair* ‘my father’, *t’ainm* / *th’ainm* ‘thy name’ and *mo chuilen* ‘my puppy’.

| Combination | Fused form |
|---|---|
| do + mo | **dom** ‘to my’ |

| do + ar | **dar** ‘to our’ |

## One point

### Look at the mutation, not the word

Poss pronouns do not change for gender, number or case of the noun; there is one form per person. The poss *a* is the same word for ‘his, her, its, their’: the next word shows which (lenition: his, its; aspiration: her; nasalization: their). Be familiar with Old Irish spelling to recognize the mutation; nasalization and aspiration are often invisible.

**Quick answers**

- **How do I tell *a* ‘his’ from the article or the voc *a*?** The article *a* (neut nom/acc) comes before a neut noun and nasalizes; the voc *a* lenites and is followed by a noun in the voc; the poss *a* is followed by a noun with a poss meaning. Context decides.
- **Why do *do* and *di* both become *dia*?** Both prepositions give *dia* with poss *a*; context decides.
- **What do posss do in the gen-like function?** They also replace a gen: *my book* = ‘the book that belongs to me’ (§12.4).
