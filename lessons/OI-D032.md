## Paradigm

### Independent and dependent forms

| Verb type | Form | Phrase |
|---|---|---|
| simple verb (*scaraid*) | **scaraid** (abs ending) | **ní·scara** (conj ending) |
| compound verb (*do·scara*) | **do·scara** (deut) | **ní·tascra** (prot) |
| compound verb (*do·beir*) | **do·beir** (deut) | **ní·tabair** (prot) |
| compound verb (*ad·rími*) | **ad·rími** (deut) | **ní·airmi** (prot) |

*Form* = independent position; *Phrase* = dependent position, after *ní*.

## One point

### The stress moves left after a particle

Every verbal form in Old Irish has two variants: an independent and a dependent one. In simple verbs the difference is made by abs versus conj endings. In compound verbs only conj endings exist, so the stress does the work (Stifter §15.2):

- **independent position**: stress on the second element, the first preverb comes right before it (**deuterotonic**, abbreviated deut, from Greek *deuteros* ‘second’);
- **dependent position**: after any other conj particle (*ní*, *in*, …), the stress shifts one position to the left, onto the first element (**prototonic**, abbreviated prot, from Greek *protos* ‘first’).

The shift has heavy consequences: the prot form usually has one more syllable, so a different syncope pattern applies and the two forms can look very different (Day 33). The element right before the stress is called pretonic.

**Quick answers**

- **Which form is the dictionary form?** Stifter gives both the deut and prot 3sg of every compound verb.
- **What about a compound verb with several particles?** The first particle always comes before the stress.
- **Is the pretonic preverb written separately?** Yes, with the raised dot.
