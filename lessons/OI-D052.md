### Deciding the stem class from the nominative singular

| Ending of the adjective | Class | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-e** | io-, ia-adjective | **buide** ‘yellow’ |
| a palatalized consonant | i-adjective | **maith** ‘good’ |
| a **u** before the final consonant | u-adjective | **dub** ‘black’ |
| a non-palatalized consonant (otherwise) | o-, a-adjective | **bán** ‘white’ |

## One point

### Four classes, one test

Only a few adjectives are u-stems: *dub*, *tiug* ‘thick’, *fliuch* ‘wet’, *accus* / *ocus* ‘near’ and some compounds such as *solus* ‘bright’, *follus* ‘clear’, *cumung* ‘narrow’, *diriuch* ‘straight’. Vocatives are not attested. The class of any adjective follows from its nominative singular; apply the four rules in order: *-e* → io-/ia-, palatalized consonant → i-, *u* before the final consonant → u-, anything else (a non-palatalized consonant) → o-/a-. A compound like *solus* shows syncope in the endings (*soilsi*, feminine genitive *soilse*).

**Quick answers**

- **Why are the genitive plurals endingless?** In u-stem adjectives the genitive plural behaves like the o-, a-adjectives (Day 11).
- **Why do the singular masculine forms look like nouns?** They inflect like o-/a-adjectives with u-infection.
- **Is the test reliable?** Yes, for the nominative singular; it is the quickest way to find the paradigm.
