## Paradigm

### *caraid* ‘loves’ · W1 · present indicative · singular

| Person | Absolute form | Conjunct form after ní |
|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | **caraimm** ‘I love’ | **ní·caraimm** |
| 2nd sg. | **carai** ‘thou lovest’ | **ní·carai** |
| 3rd sg. | **caraid** ‘(s)he loves’ | **ní·cara** |

W1 stands for the first weak class (McCone’s classification). The root is *car-*; the root-final consonant stays non-palatalized in the whole paradigm.

### The same pattern with other W1 verbs

| Person | *legaid* ‘reads’ (absolute) | *legaid* (conjunct after ní) | *móraid* ‘praises’ (absolute) | *móraid* (conjunct after ní) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | **legaimm** | **ní·legaimm** | **móraimm** | **ní·móraimm** |
| 2nd sg. | **legai** | **ní·legai** | **mórai** | **ní·mórai** |
| 3rd sg. | **legaid** | **ní·lega** | **móraid** | **ní·móra** |

## One point

### Absolute or conjunct: look at what stands before the verb

A verb with nothing before it takes **absolute** endings (*caraid*); as soon as a particle or preverb stands before the root, the verb takes **conjunct** endings (*ní·cara*). The person is shown only by the ending; there are no subject pronouns (Stifter §8.3), so *caraid* means ‘he, she or it loves’ according to context.

**Quick answers**

- **Why the dot in ní·cara?** It marks the end of the particle; the stress falls right after it. Other books write ní-cara, ní:cara or ní cara (§8.1).
- **Why is there a double m in caraimm?** The 1st singular ending is unlenited, but it is usually written with a single *m* (§8.4 note 3).
- **What are W1 verbs?** Many are made from nouns and adjectives (*marb* ‘dead’ → *marbaid* ‘kills’, *mór* ‘big’ → *móraid* ‘praises’), and many Latin loans belong here (*scríbaid* ‘writes’, *legaid* ‘reads’) (§8.4).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development

1. W1 is mostly a denominative class: the verb means ‘make like the noun or adjective’ (*marbaid* ‘makes dead’ = ‘kills’).
2. The origin of the absolute and conjunct endings is taught on Day 24.
