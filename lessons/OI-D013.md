## Paradigm

**Abbreviations:** nom nominative · gen genitive · prep prepositional (dative, dat, in other grammars) · acc accusative · voc vocative · sg singular · pl plural · masc masculine · fem feminine · neut neuter · 1sg, 2sg, 3sg, 1pl, 2pl, 3pl = 1st, 2nd, 3rd person sg or pl · abs absolute · conj conjunct · deut deuterotonic · prot prototonic · pres present · pret preterite · fut future · cond conditional · impf imperfect · ind indicative · subj subjunctive · impv imperative · pass passive.

### *caraid* ‘loves’ · W1 · pres ind · sg

| Person | Abs form | Conj form after ní |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | **caraimm** ‘I love’ | **ní·caraimm** |
| 2sg | **carai** ‘thou lovest’ | **ní·carai** |
| 3sg | **caraid** ‘(s)he loves’ | **ní·cara** |

W1 stands for the first weak class (McCone’s classification). The root is *car-*; the root-final consonant stays non-palatalized in the whole paradigm.

### The same pattern with other W1 verbs

| Person | *legaid* ‘reads’ (abs) | *legaid* (conj after ní) | *móraid* ‘praises’ (abs) | *móraid* (conj after ní) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1sg | **legaimm** | **ní·legaimm** | **móraimm** | **ní·móraimm** |
| 2sg | **legai** | **ní·legai** | **mórai** | **ní·mórai** |
| 3sg | **legaid** | **ní·lega** | **móraid** | **ní·móra** |

## One point

### Abs or conj: look at what stands before the verb

A verb with nothing before it takes **abs** endings (*caraid*); as soon as a particle or preverb stands before the root, the verb takes **conj** endings (*ní·cara*). The person is shown only by the ending; there are no subject pronouns (Stifter §8.3), so *caraid* means ‘he, she or it loves’ according to context.

**Quick answers**

- **Why the dot in ní·cara?** It marks the end of the particle; the stress falls right after it. Other books write ní-cara, ní:cara or ní cara (§8.1).
- **Why is there a double m in caraimm?** The 1sg ending is unlenited, but it is usually written with a single *m* (§8.4 note 3).
- **What are W1 verbs?** Many are made from nouns and adjectives (*marb* ‘dead’ → *marbaid* ‘kills’, *mór* ‘big’ → *móraid* ‘praises’), and many Latin loans belong here (*scríbaid* ‘writes’, *legaid* ‘reads’) (§8.4).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development

1. W1 is mostly a denominative class: the verb means ‘make like the noun or adjective’ (*marbaid* ‘makes dead’ = ‘kills’).
