## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the article itself changes.

### The article ‘the’ · masc · pl (before b, d, g, p, t, c, m)

| Case | Pl article + effect | Example phrase |
|---|---|---|
| Nom | **in** [L] | **in chaitt** ‘the cats’ |
| Gen | **inna** [N] | **inna catt** ‘of the cats’ |
| Prep | **-(s)naib** [0] | **donaib cattaib** ‘to the cats’ |
| Acc | **inna** [H] | **inna chattu** ‘the cats’ (object) |
| Voc | **a** [L] (particle; no article) | **a chattu** ‘O cats’ |

### The variants of the article (sg and pl)

| The next word begins with | Nom sg | Gen sg and nom pl | Prep sg |
|---|---|---|---|
| b, d, g, p, t, c, m | **in** | **in** | **-(s)in** |
| a vowel | **int** | **ind** | **-(s)ind** |
| l, r, n, f | **in** | **ind** | **-(s)ind** |
| s | **in** | **int** (s > h) | **-(s)int** |

Examples (Stifter §7.2): *int en* ‘the bird’, *ind euin* ‘of the bird, the birds’, *ind rein* ‘of the sea’, *int sacairt* ‘of the priest, the priests’, *dond eun* ‘to the bird’, *dont sacart* ‘to the priest’.

## One point

### The pl article is again the o-stem pattern

The mutations of the pl article are those of the o-stem pl: the nom pl lenites [L], the gen pl nasalizes [N], the prep pl has no effect [0], the acc pl aspirates [H]. The variants *int / ind* only depend on the first sound of the next word.

**Quick answers**

- **Why is *inna* sometimes written *na*?** In the oldest texts *inna* has two syllables; the most common form is *na* (§7.1 note 5).
- **Why *ind* before a vowel and before *l, r, n, f*?** Stifter lists these sounds without a reason. Toner’s study of the article in place-names (*Nomina* 22) confirms the pattern for the gen: the older *-d* was kept before vowels and before lenited *f, r, l, n*; he calls the *-d* “original” but does not say where it comes from. The pattern was given up by the twelfth century. No source I could consult explains why exactly these sounds keep the *d*, so treat the list as a rule to learn.
- **Is the voc pl with an article?** No: the article cannot be used in the voc.

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: the pl article

| Case | Proto-Celtic (Stifter) | Old Irish |
|---|---|---|
| Nom | \*(s)indi | **in** [L] |
| Prep | \*(s)indobis | **-(s)naib** [0] |

1. As with the noun, the prep pl ending is \*-obis (Day 2).
2. Stifter’s other pl forms are garbled in the text layer and are not given here.

**Summary.** The forms are the regular ones for an o-stem; the details of the variants are Stifter’s descriptive rules. Unresolved: why only these initial sounds take *ind*.
