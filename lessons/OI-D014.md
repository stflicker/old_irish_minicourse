## Paradigm

### *caraid* ‘loves’ · W1 · present indicative · plural

| Person | Absolute form | Conjunct form after ní |
|---|---|---|
| 1st pl. | **carmai** ‘we love’ | **ní·caram** |
| 2nd pl. | **carthae** ‘you (all) love’ | **ní·caraid** |
| 3rd pl. | **carait** ‘they love’ | **ní·carat** |

### The same pattern with *glanaid* ‘cleans’ (root *glan-*)

| Person | Absolute form | Conjunct form after ní |
|---|---|---|
| 1st pl. | **glanmai** | **ní·glanam** |
| 2nd pl. | **glantae** | **ní·glanaid** |
| 3rd pl. | **glanait** | **ní·glanat** |

### Identify the form

| Form | Person and number | Series |
|---|---|---|
| **carmai** | 1st plural | absolute |
| **ní·caraid** | 2nd plural | conjunct |
| **caraid** | 3rd singular | absolute |
| **ní·carat** | 3rd plural | conjunct |

## One point

### Spelling and ending can overlap; the particle decides

*caraid* is the 3rd singular absolute; *ní·caraid* is the 2nd plural conjunct. The ending is the same, the series is shown by what stands before the verb. In the 1st plural the absolute *-mai* has an unlenited *m*, the conjunct *-am* a lenited *m*: both are usually written with a plain *m* (§8.4 note 4).

**Quick answers**

- **What are ní and in?** The two most important particles: *ní* ‘not’ and *in* (which makes a statement a question). Both take the conjunct form of the verb (§9.1).
- **Why *glantae*?** After *n* the ending *-thae* becomes *-tae* (§8.4 note 5).
- **Why do I need only the singular and plural separately?** Each session teaches one number; together they give the six persons.
