## Paradigm

### *caraid* ‘loves’ · W1 · pres ind · pl

| Person | Abs form | Conj form after ní |
|---|---|---|
| 1pl | **carmai** ‘we love’ | **ní·caram** |
| 2pl | **carthae** ‘you (all) love’ | **ní·caraid** |
| 3pl | **carait** ‘they love’ | **ní·carat** |

### The same pattern with *glanaid* ‘cleans’ (root *glan-*)

| Person | Abs form | Conj form after ní |
|---|---|---|
| 1pl | **glanmai** | **ní·glanam** |
| 2pl | **glantae** | **ní·glanaid** |
| 3pl | **glanait** | **ní·glanat** |

### Identify the form

| Form | Person and number | Series |
|---|---|---|
| **carmai** | 1pl | abs |
| **ní·caraid** | 2pl | conj |
| **caraid** | 3sg | abs |
| **ní·carat** | 3pl | conj |

## One point

### Spelling and ending can overlap; the particle decides

*caraid* is the 3sg abs; *ní·caraid* is the 2pl conj. The ending is the same, the series is shown by what stands before the verb. In the 1pl the abs *-mai* has an unlenited *m*, the conj *-am* a lenited *m*: both are usually written with a plain *m* (§8.4 note 4).

**Quick answers**

- **What are ní and in?** The two most important particles: *ní* ‘not’ and *in* (which makes a statement a question). Both take the conj form of the verb (§9.1).
- **Why *glantae*?** After *n* the ending *-thae* becomes *-tae* (§8.4 note 5).
- **Why do I need only the sg and pl separately?** Each session teaches one number; together they give the six persons.
