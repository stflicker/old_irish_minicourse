| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **in chethramad rann** | the fourth part |
| **in seissed lebor** | the sixth book |
| **cetadaig** | first night |
| **cetmuinter** | first wife |
| **ind aile ingen deac** | the twelfth girl |
| **in tress lóech deac** | the thirteenth warrior |

## One point

### Adjective endings, noun-first order

Ordinals are adjectives in agreement with their noun in case, number and gender: *cétnae*, *aile* and *tánaise* are io-/ia-adjectives, all others o-/a-adjectives. But unlike normal adjectives they stand before the word they qualify: *in chethramad rann*. The exceptions are *cét-*, which is compounded with its noun (*cetadaig* ‘first night’), and *tánaise* and occasionally *aile*, which follow. As with the cardinals, 12–19 add *deac* after the noun. Stifter points out that much restructuring has taken place in these words and gives Proto-Celtic forms like \*kintu- for ‘first’ (compare Gaulish *Cintugnatos* ‘first born’).

**Quick answers**

- **Why two words for ‘second’?** *Tánaise* and *aile* both exist; *aile* also means ‘other’ (Day 34).
- **What does *cetnae etir chomtrromma* mean?** ‘first among equals’ (Latin *primus inter pares*), Stifter’s illustration.
- **Do ordinals mutate?** The article does, as for any adjective.
