## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

## One point

### Long and short variants

The fem i-stems are closely connected with the ia-stems: the endings are largely identical, and the two declensions influenced each other. In most forms an extra syllable is added to the root; where nothing is added the root-final consonant is palatalized. Two variants occur. The **long** variant (*rígain*, *adaig* ‘night’, *bliadain* ‘year’, *Brigit*, *inis* ‘island’, *seitig* ‘wife’) probably continues the inherited pattern. The **short** variant (*aithis*, *buith* ‘being’, loanwords from Latin) has no ending in the prep and acc sg and is probably secondary, influenced by the a- and i-declensions.

**Quick answers**

- **Is this the same as the i-stems of Day 43?** No: those are a different declension (masc, fem and neut nouns with a PIE *-i-* stem).
- **What gives *rígnae*?** The long variant loses the vowel before the ending by syncope; whether the consonant is palatalized depends on the vowel that was lost.
- **Are there ī-stem adjectives?** No.

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development

The Old Irish ī-stem declension basically continues the PIE fem suffix \*-ih₂ (the so-called devī-inflection). The nom pl is hard to reconstruct: Stifter discusses three possible explanations (taken over from the i-stems, replaced by the consonant-stem ending \*-es, or replaced by the acc pl as in the a-stems).
