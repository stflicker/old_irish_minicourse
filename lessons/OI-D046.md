## Paradigm

### *ar* ‘for, on account of, in front of’ · prep or acc

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| 1sg | **airium**, **erum** |
| 2sg | **airiut**, **erut** |
| 3sg masc, neut, prep | **airiu** |
| 3sg masc, neut, acc | **airi** |
| 3sg fem, prep | – |
| 3sg fem, acc | **airre** |
| 1pl | **erunn**, **eronn** |
| 2pl | **airib**, **eruib** |
| 3pl, prep | **airib** |
| 3pl, acc | **airriu**, **erru** |

### Other prepositions in the same pattern

| Preposition | 1sg | 3sg masc/neut | 3pl |
|---|---|---|---|
| *la* ‘with’ | **lem(m)** | **leis(s)** | **leu**, **leo** |

| *tre* ‘through’ | **trium** | **triit** | **treu** |

## One point

### Four prepositions, two case-sets

*Ar*, *fo*, *for* and *in* are the four prepositions that can take both cases: the prep expresses position, the acc direction. In the conjugated forms the 1st and 2nd persons are the same for both, while the 3rd persons always differ: *indib* ‘in them’, *intiu* ‘into them’. A few forms are not attested (the 3rd fem prep of *ar* and *fo*). Remember that all the conjugated prepositions have to be learned individually (Day 38).

**Quick answers**

- **Do *la*, *fri* and *tre* take one case?** Yes: the acc, so only one set of 3rd person forms exists.
- **What about 3pl -u and -ib?** The acc takes -u (*friu*, *forru*), the prep -ib (*indib*, *foraib*).
- **Which forms were used in the oldest texts?** Stifter gives variants; the shorter forms are younger.
