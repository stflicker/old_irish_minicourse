## Paradigm

### Nominal prepositions (all take the gen)

| Phrase | Meaning | Example with a poss |
|---|---|---|
| **dochum** | towards | **mo dochum** ‘towards me’ |
| **i n-alladi** | near, beside | **it alladi** ‘beside you’ |
| **fo bíth** | because of, for the sake of | **a bíth** ‘for his sake’ |
| **i ndegaid** | after, behind | **inna degaid** ‘after her’ |
| **ar belaib** | before, in front of | **ara belaib** ‘in front of it’ |
| **ar chiunn** | facing, awaiting | – |
| **ar chenn** | towards, against | – |
| **tar cenn** | for | **tara cenn** ‘for them’ |
| **iar cúl** | behind (position) | **iarmo chúl** ‘behind me’ |
| **for cúl** | behind (direction) | **fort chúlu** ‘behind you’ |
| **i ndead**, **i ndiad** | after | **inna dead** ‘after him’ |
| **tar éis** | behind, for, instead of | **tara éissi** ‘behind her’ |
| **i mmedón** | in the middle | **inna medón** ‘in its midst’ |

### Dem particles

| Phrase | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **in lebor-sa** | this book | -so, -sa after npal sounds |
| **ind libuir-se** | these books | -se, -sea after pal sounds |
| **in ben-sin** | that woman | -sin for ‘that’, more remote or already mentioned |
| **ind fir-sin** | those men | – |

## One point

### Prepositions made from nouns, and a ‘this/that’ made from particles

A **secondary** (nominal) preposition is built from a noun: *dochum* is a petrified case form of *tochim* ‘stepping forward’, *i ndegaid* is *in* + the prep of *degaid* ‘search’. English *in front of* is a parallel. Unlike primary prepositions they take the gen, the article of the following word does not merge with them, and they cannot take personal endings: instead the poss pronoun comes right before the noun (with its mutation): *mo dochum*. **Enclitic** means that a word is never accented and follows the word it qualifies. Old Irish has no dem words like *this* and *that*; it uses the enclitic particles *-so/-sa/-se* ‘this’ (always with unlenited *s*) and *-sin* ‘that’, always with the article and written here with a hyphen. Alone they act as nouns: *in-so* ‘this’, *sin* ‘that’, *amal sin* ‘like that’.

**Quick answers**

- **Does the article fuse with a nominal preposition?** No.
- **Where do I find more of them?** In GOI 536–537 and in DIL under the noun.
- **Do the particles lenite?** No: the *s* stays unlenited.

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development

Nominal prepositions were formed only recently in the prehistory or history of Irish; they are not inherited from Proto-Celtic or Proto-Indo-European.
