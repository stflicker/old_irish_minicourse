## Paradigm

**Abbreviations:** nom nominative · gen genitive · prep prepositional (dative, dat, in other grammars) · acc accusative · voc vocative · sg singular · pl plural · masc masculine · fem feminine · neut neuter · 1sg, 2sg, 3sg, 1pl, 2pl, 3pl = 1st, 2nd, 3rd person sg or pl · abs absolute · conj conjunct · deut deuterotonic · prot prototonic · pres present · pret preterite · fut future · cond conditional · impf imperfect · ind indicative · subj subjunctive · impv imperative · pass passive.

### *do·beir* ‘gives, brings’ · S1 compound (to- + ber-)

| Person | Form | Phrase |
|---|---|---|
| 1sg | **do·biur** | **ní·tabur** |
| 2sg | **do·bir** | **ní·tabair** |
| 3sg | **do·beir** | **ní·tabair** |
| 1pl | **do·beram** | **ní·taibrem** |
| 2pl | **do·beirid** | **ní·taibrid** |
| 3pl | **do·berat** | **ní·taibret** |

*Form* = independent (deut); *Phrase* = dependent (prot), after *ní*.

## One point

### Same rules as before, with two extra effects

Compound S1 verbs behave exactly like the classes already learned: only conj endings, deut versus prot by stress (Day 32). Two points are new: (1) raising and lowering of the root vowel usually does not apply in unstressed syllables, so it can be ignored for the root in prot forms (though it may apply to the preverb under the stress); (2) syncope in the pl prot forms can secondarily palatalize the root-final consonant, as in *ní·taibret*. Even in the earliest texts the 1sg may end in *-u* or *-(a)imm* and the 2sg in *-i* to avoid confusion with the 3sg (*in·iccu*, *in·iccimm* ‘I reach’).

**Quick answers**

- **Is the 2sg prot distinct from the 3sg?** In this table no, they are the same form; restructurings sometimes separate them.
- **How is the compound derived?** Preverb *to-* + root *ber-*: *do·beir*, prot *·tabair*.
- **Where do I practice?** Conjugate *as·beir* ‘says’, *do·icc* ‘comes’, *for·beir* ‘increases’ (Stifter exercise 18.5).
