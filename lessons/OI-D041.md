## Paradigm

### *do·beir* ‘gives, brings’ · S1 compound (to- + ber-)

| Person | Form | Phrase |
|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | **do·biur** | **ní·tabur** |
| 2nd sg. | **do·bir** | **ní·tabair** |
| 3rd sg. | **do·beir** | **ní·tabair** |
| 1st pl. | **do·beram** | **ní·taibrem** |
| 2nd pl. | **do·beirid** | **ní·taibrid** |
| 3rd pl. | **do·berat** | **ní·taibret** |

*Form* = independent (deuterotonic); *Phrase* = dependent (prototonic), after *ní*.

## One point

### Same rules as before, with two extra effects

Compound S1 verbs behave exactly like the classes already learned: only conjunct endings, deuterotonic versus prototonic by stress (Day 32). Two points are new: (1) raising and lowering of the root vowel usually does not apply in unstressed syllables, so it can be ignored for the root in prototonic forms (though it may apply to the preverb under the stress); (2) syncope in the plural prototonic forms can secondarily palatalize the root-final consonant, as in *ní·taibret*. Even in the earliest texts the 1st singular may end in *-u* or *-(a)imm* and the 2nd singular in *-i* to avoid confusion with the 3rd singular (*in·iccu*, *in·iccimm* ‘I reach’).

**Quick answers**

- **Is the 2nd sg. prototonic distinct from the 3rd sg.?** In this table no, they are the same form; restructurings sometimes separate them.
- **How is the compound derived?** Preverb *to-* + root *ber-*: *do·beir*, prototonic *·tabair*.
- **Where do I practice?** Conjugate *as·beir* ‘says’, *do·icc* ‘comes’, *for·beir* ‘increases’ (Stifter exercise 18.5).
