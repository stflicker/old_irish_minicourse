## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the article itself changes.

### The article ‘the’ · neuter · singular

| Case | Singular article + effect | Example phrase |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **a** [N] | **a cenn** ‘the head’ |
| Genitive | **in** [L] | **in chinn** ‘of the head’ |
| Prepositional | **-(s)in** [L] | **don chiunn** ‘to the head’ |
| Accusative | **a** [N] | **a cenn** ‘the head’ (object) |
| Vocative | **a** [L] (particle; no article) | **a chenn** ‘O head’ |

### The article ‘the’ · neuter · plural

| Case | Plural article + effect | Example phrase |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **inna** [0] | **inna cenn / inna cenna** ‘the heads’ |
| Genitive | **inna** [N] | **inna cenn** ‘of the heads’ |
| Prepositional | **-(s)naib** [0] | **donaib cennaib** ‘to the heads’ |
| Accusative | **inna** [0] | **inna cenn / inna cenna** ‘the heads’ (object) |
| Vocative | **a** [L] (particle; no article) | **a chenna** ‘O heads’ |

The short plural *cenn* is lenited [L] after the nominative and accusative article; the long plural *cenna* [H] (Day 5).

## One point

### Only the nominative and accusative differ from the masculine

The neuter article is the masculine article in all cases except the nominative and accusative, singular and plural (Stifter §7.3). Its effects match those of the neuter o-stem noun (Day 4): the singular nasalizes, the genitive lenites. So *a cenn* (neuter) and *in catt* (masculine) are the two basic shapes of “the + noun”.

**Quick answers**

- **How do I tell the article *a* from other *a* words?** The article *a* (neuter) stands before a noun and nasalizes it. The vocative particle *a* lenites. The possessive *a* ‘his, her, its, their’ has its own mutations (Day 25).
- **Which prepositions fuse with the neuter accusative?** The same ones as in the masculine; the s-rule is the same: *fo* + *a* gives *fon* (no s), *i* + *a* gives *isa(n)* (with s) (§7.3 note 2).
- **Is inna always two syllables?** No, only in the oldest texts (§7.3 note 3).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: the neuter article

| Case | Proto-Celtic (Stifter) | Old Irish |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative singular | \*(s)osim | **a** [N] |
| Genitive singular | \*(s)indi | **in** [L] |

1. The nominative and accusative singular come from a different pronoun form than the masculine \*(s)indos: Stifter writes \*(s)osim. That is why the neuter article looks so different.
2. The rest is the same as in the masculine article.

**Summary.** The genitive and prepositional are regular; the neuter nominative and accusative continue a different pronoun form. Unresolved: the exact Proto-Celtic form of the neuter plural (Stifter marks it with a question mark).
