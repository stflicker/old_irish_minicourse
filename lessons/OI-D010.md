## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the article itself changes.

### The article ‘the’ · neut · sg

| Case | Sg article + effect | Example phrase |
|---|---|---|
| Nom | **a** [N] | **a cenn** ‘the head’ |
| Gen | **in** [L] | **in chinn** ‘of the head’ |
| Prep | **-(s)in** [L] | **don chiunn** ‘to the head’ |
| Acc | **a** [N] | **a cenn** ‘the head’ (object) |
| Voc | **a** [L] (particle; no article) | **a chenn** ‘O head’ |

### The article ‘the’ · neut · pl

| Case | Pl article + effect | Example phrase |
|---|---|---|
| Nom | **inna** [0] | **inna cenn / inna cenna** ‘the heads’ |
| Gen | **inna** [N] | **inna cenn** ‘of the heads’ |
| Prep | **-(s)naib** [0] | **donaib cennaib** ‘to the heads’ |
| Acc | **inna** [0] | **inna cenn / inna cenna** ‘the heads’ (object) |
| Voc | **a** [L] (particle; no article) | **a chenna** ‘O heads’ |

The short pl *cenn* is lenited [L] after the nom and acc article; the long pl *cenna* [H] (Day 5).

## One point

### Only the nom and acc differ from the masc

The neut article is the masc article in all cases except the nom and acc, sg and pl (Stifter §7.3). Its effects match those of the neut o-stem noun (Day 4): the sg nasalizes, the gen lenites. So *a cenn* (neut) and *in catt* (masc) are the two basic shapes of “the + noun”.

**Quick answers**

- **How do I tell the article *a* from other *a* words?** The article *a* (neut) stands before a noun and nasalizes it. The voc particle *a* lenites. The poss *a* ‘his, her, its, their’ has its own mutations (Day 25).
- **Which prepositions fuse with the neut acc?** The same ones as in the masc; the s-rule is the same: *fo* + *a* gives *fon* (no s), *i* + *a* gives *isa(n)* (with s) (§7.3 note 2).
- **Is inna always two syllables?** No, only in the oldest texts (§7.3 note 3).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: the neut article

| Case | Proto-Celtic (Stifter) | Old Irish |
|---|---|---|
| Nom sg | \*(s)osim | **a** [N] |
| Gen sg | \*(s)indi | **in** [L] |

1. The nom and acc sg come from a different pronoun form than the masc \*(s)indos: Stifter writes \*(s)osim. That is why the neut article looks so different.
2. The rest is the same as in the masc article.

**Summary.** The gen and prep are regular; the neut nom and acc continue a different pronoun form. Unresolved: the exact Proto-Celtic form of the neut pl (Stifter marks it with a question mark).
