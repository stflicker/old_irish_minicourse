## Paradigm
| Neuter context | Article | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nom./acc. singular | **a** (N) | **a cenn** ‘the head’ |
| Nom./acc. singular (visible nasal) | **a** (N) | **a ndliged** ‘the law’ |
| Genitive singular | **in** (L) | **in chinn** ‘of the head’ |
| Prep. singular after *do* | **don** (L) | **don chiunn** ‘for the head’ |
| Nom./acc. plural | **inna** | **inna cenn** ‘the heads’ (short plural) |

Neuter *a* in this table is the **definite article**, not yet the possessive ‘his/her/their’.
## One point
The standout neuter form is **a** in **both nominative and accusative singular**. This exactly matches the neuter noun's nominative–accusative syncretism.

An example with a visible nasal marker is *a ndliged* ‘the law’. With *cenn*, Stifter writes **a cenn**; an N effect is nonetheless assigned to the phrase, even if not separately spelled.

All the other article cases largely follow the masculine pattern. In the plural, *inna* also covers nominative and accusative.

*Source:* Stifter (2006), §7.3 and the two paradigms preceding §7.4.
