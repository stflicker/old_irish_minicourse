## Paradigm
| Case | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **ben** | **mná** |
| Genitive | **mná** | **ban** |
| Prepositional | **mnái** | **mnáib** |
| Accusative | **mnái** (older **bein**) | **mná** |
| Vocative | **a ben** | **a mná** |

Forms are cited without detailed following-word markers here so you can focus first on the striking alternation **ben / mná / ban**.
## One point

### Read the forms: function → change

**ben** is nominative singular ‘woman’; **mná** can be genitive singular or nominative plural. The irregular paradigm changes the root, not just the ending.

Do not try to derive **mná** by mechanically adding an ending to **ben**. This word has a **historically irregular** paradigm: *ben* in the nominative singular, *mná* in the genitive singular and nominative plural, *ban* in the genitive plural.

Stifter notes that the **accusative singular mnái** replaced the historically expected **bein** quite early. That is a change of paradigm shape, not merely spelling variation.

A good reading strategy is to recognize **mná** at first sight as potentially genitive singular, nominative plural or accusative plural. The article and syntax will tell you which.

*Source:* Stifter (2006), §10.3, *ben* and its variant forms.

## Comparison (optional)

### 1. One old word, multiple stem grades

Old Irish **ben** ‘woman’ is ultimately connected with an Indo-European word family usually reconstructed around ***gʷenh₂-***. Compare Greek **gynḗ** ‘woman’ and related Indo-European forms. These are **lexical cognates**, but the **inflectional classes and individual case endings** need not be identical in the languages.

### 2. The Old Irish paradigm is not simple suffixation

| Old Irish form | Function | Morphological point |
|---|---|---|
| **ben** | nom. sg. | familiar citation form |
| **mná** | gen. sg. and nom./acc. pl. | a **different stem shape** generalized across cells |
| **ban** | gen. pl. | another inherited/restructured stem shape |
| **mnái** | acc./prep. sg. | an oblique form reinforced by **analogy** |

The startling **b- versus mn-** alternation comes from the complex Celtic development of this old word and the relationship among historical stem variants. It should **not be treated as a productive rule** that transforms every *b-* into *mn-*.

### 3. What is demonstrably innovative?

The **accusative singular *mnái*** replaced an older expected ***bein*** in the history of Irish. This is **paradigmatic leveling**: an oblique stem spreads into a slot where another stem had originally been expected. Forms that began with different phonological histories thus get redistributed into a new **grammatical pattern**.

Distinguish **suppletion** (unrelated roots within one paradigm) from the present situation: *ben/mná* belong to an **historically connected word family**, despite their surface difference.

**References:** Stifter (2006), §10.3; Thurneysen, *A Grammar of Old Irish*, irregular feminine nouns; comparative etymology of *ben*.
