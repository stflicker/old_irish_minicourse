## Paradigm

Mutation of the next word: [L] lenition · [N] nasalization · [H] aspiration (h- before a vowel, gemination of a consonant) · [0] none. The letters show how the *next* word is mutated, not how the noun itself changes.

### *ben* ‘woman’ · feminine, historically an a-stem

| Case | Singular form + effect | Plural form + effect |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | **ben** [L] | **mná** [H] |
| Genitive | **mná** [H] | **ban** [N] |
| Prepositional | **mnaí** [L] | **mnáib** [0] |
| Accusative | **mnaí** [N] | **mná** [H] |
| Vocative | **a ben** [L] | **a mná** [H] |

The old accusative singular *bein* [N] was replaced by *mnaí* very early. The prepositional plural *mnáib* was originally disyllabic (*mnaaib*).

## One point

### Three stems in one word

*ben* has the stem *ben-* (nominative, vocative singular), *mn-* (all other singular forms and the nominative, accusative, vocative, prepositional plural), and *ban* (genitive plural; also in compounds). The word historically belongs to the a-stems, but the Old Irish paradigm is anomalous (Stifter §10.3). Learn it as a list.

**Quick answers**

- **Is there a form without gender?** In legal texts a neutral form *be* occurs, e.g. in *be cuitgemsa* ‘woman of joint authority’.
- **How do compounds work?** *ban-* makes female nouns: *banab* ‘abbess’ from *ab* ‘abbot’, *bantigerna* ‘lady’ from *tigerna* ‘lord’, *banchomairle* ‘a woman’s advice’.
- **Which forms are easy to confuse?** Genitive singular, nominative plural and accusative plural are all *mná*; only the mutation of the next word or the sentence helps.

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development: why is *ben* irregular?

| Case | PIE (Stifter) | Old Irish |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative singular | \*gʷenh₂ | **ben** [L] |
| Genitive singular | \*gʷneh₂s | **mná** [H] |

1. The PIE word for ‘woman’ had a stem with a full grade (\*gʷen-) and a reduced grade (\*gʷn-) in different cases. Irish kept both: *ben-* and *mn-* (the initial cluster \*gʷn- ended up as *mn-*).
2. The same root gives Greek *gynḗ* ‘woman’ and English *queen*.

**Summary.** The alternation is inherited, not an Irish accident. Regular sound change: the vowel alternation. Analogy («): *mnaí* and the prepositional plural. Unresolved: the exact PIE shape of several forms (garbled in the text layer, so only two forms are given).
