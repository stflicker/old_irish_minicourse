## Paradigm

### Two series of endings · present, with the reconstructed Insular Celtic stage

| Person | Abs form | Conj form after ní | Insular Celtic (abs → conj) |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1 3sg | **caraid** | **ní·cara** | \*-ati → \*-at |
| W1 3pl | **carait** | **ní·carat** | \*-anti → \*-ant |
| W2 3sg | **leicid** | **ní·leici** | \*-īti → \*-īt |
| W2 3pl | **leicit** | **ní·leicet** | \*-inti → \*-int |

Stifter’s tables 12.2 and 12.3 give these Insular Celtic forms; they are rough. The pre-Celtic stage (e.g. W1 3sg \*-ah₂(i̯e)ti) is shown in the same tables.

## One point

### What is known, what is debated

**Known (Stifter §12.3):**
1. The abs endings (with the final \*-i) and the conj endings (without it) differ exactly by a final short \*-i.
2. This distinction is *not* the PIE contrast between primary and secondary endings; Old Welsh has a parallel.
3. The ending \*-i was lost early in general, but kept before certain particles, and the forms with and without it were then redistributed through the whole verbal system.

**Debated:** the exact nature of the enclitic particles in front of which \*-i was retained is, in Stifter’s words, “a matter of heated disputes”.

**Quick answers**

- **Why does the 1sg have -imm in all classes?** The *m* was delenited on the model of the copula *am(m)* ‘I am’ and then spread, replacing the older -u (§12.3 note 1).
- **Why is the W1 2sg conj -ai?** It was probably taken over from the abs ending (note 2).
- **Why is the 1pl abs -mai with an unlenited m?** On the model of the copula *ammi* ‘we are’ (note 3); the conj -am keeps the expected lenited form.
- **Is W1 one PIE class?** No: at least three PIE formations fell together in W1 (note 4); in W2, at least two (note 5).

## Comparison (optional)

### Historical development

The tables here are the development; the open question is the particles. Stifter says that reconstructing verbal forms is much harder than reconstructing noun forms and that his tables convey only a very rough picture.

**Summary.** Stated by Stifter: the Insular Celtic origin and the role of the lost \*-i. Debated: which particles preserved it. Unresolved: the details of the redistribution.
