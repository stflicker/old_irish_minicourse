# Reference: abbreviations, marks, palatalization

Shown (collapsed) at the top of every lesson page; the build wraps each abbreviation in small capitals with a hover tooltip. In the Markdown lessons the abbreviations are written in plain lowercase. Keep this file, `GLOSS`/`REFERENCE` in `scripts/table_tools.py` and `GL` in `docs/app.js` in step (a test checks the last two).

## Cases

- **nom**: nominative: the subject
- **gen**: genitive: of ...
- **prep**: prepositional: the case after many prepositions; Stifter’s name for what other grammars call the dative (dat)
- **acc**: accusative: the object
- **voc**: vocative: direct address

## Number, gender, person

- **sg, pl**: singular, plural
- **masc, fem, neut**: masculine, feminine, neuter
- **1sg, 2sg, 3sg, 1pl, 2pl, 3pl**: 1st, 2nd, 3rd person singular or plural

## Verb forms

- **abs**: absolute: ending used with nothing before the verb
- **conj**: conjunct: ending used after a particle or preverb
- **deut, prot**: deuterotonic (stress on the 2nd element), prototonic (stress on the 1st)
- **pres, pret, impf, fut, cond**: present, preterite, imperfect, future, conditional
- **ind, subj, impv**: indicative, subjunctive, imperative
- **pass**: passive
- **depo**: deponent: a verb with active meaning but its own endings (with an extra r)
- **denom**: denominative: a verb made from a noun or adjective
- **caus**: causative: ‘makes someone do X’
- **iter**: iterative: repeated action
- **vn**: verbal noun: the abstract noun that does the work of an infinitive
- **rel**: relative form of a verb
- **poss, dem**: possessive, demonstrative

## Marks on the next word

- **[L]**: lenition: the first consonant is softened (c, t, p become ch, th, ph; b, d, g, m, s, f are lenited too but this is often not written)
- **[N]**: nasalization: stops are voiced or get a nasal before them (c to g, t to d, p to b; b to mb, d to nd, g to ng), a vowel gets n-; often not written
- **[H]**: aspiration: h- before a vowel, doubling (gemination) of a consonant
- **[0]**: no mutation

## Palatalization

- **pal, npal**: palatalized, non-palatalized: a consonant said with the tongue raised towards the hard palate, or not (older grammars: “slender” and “broad”)
- **C′, C**: Stifter’s notation for a pal and an npal consonant in his ending tables
- **spelling**: a pal consonant is often shown by an i or e next to it (chaitt), an npal one by a, o or u (catt)
