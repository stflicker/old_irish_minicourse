# Course plan: Days 001-056

Titles and subtitles only. Days 001-007 and 012 are written lessons (approved wording, kept verbatim). Day 012 is written but its content will be widened (see the review notes below). Days 008-011 and 013-056 are plans: the lesson files for Days 008-028 still use the older wording and will be rewritten in the new style (standard terms, all five cases in each form table, one number per session) when we reach them. Days 029-056 have no lesson files yet.

Stifter section numbers refer to the 2006 contents. Day types: N noun, S syntax/small words, V verb, H historical, T text reading/review.

## Review of Days 001-028 (what changed and why)

The first 28 days are feasible: each day has one target, nouns and verbs alternate, and history comes only after the forms are known. Small changes:

- **Day 021** repeated earlier material. It is now a short cross-language comparison of the feminine a-stems, parallel to Day 007.
- **Day 024** claimed too much about a debated topic. It is now "what is known and what is debated" about the two ending series.
- **Day 025** teaches the whole possessive table and the trap that *a* means 'his/her/its/their', with a different mutation each time.
- **Day 026** teaches what is new in 13.1-13.2: how verse differs from prose (filler phrases, special wording), the question particle *innad* and 'believe in'. Only short pieces of Stifter's poem are quoted; the rest is referenced.
- **Subtitles of Days 008-028** are rewritten in plain language. Terms like "revealing cell" are gone.
- **Days 012-014** (verb road map, then W1 present singular and plural) are confirmed by the learner.
- **Day 012** has been rewritten as a structured overview: the parts of one verb form, person and number in both series, a table of tenses and moods, the sequence-of-tenses matrix, voice and non-finite forms, and the verb classes.

Days 029-056 follow Stifter's lessons 14.3-24 in order. Every day carries new material; practice is added inside a day, never as a day of its own. Stifter's readings, tests and exercise sections are used as practice inside days, and his varia sections (small new points) are used as new material.

## Terminology policy and audit (to apply when lesson content is revised)

Rule: use Stifter's own term, or the standard term of the textbooks and research literature (McCone, Thurneysen's *Grammar of Old Irish*, Strachan; Indo-European terms as in Fortson or Weiss); never a name of ours; explain each technical term once, at first use, in a short plain sentence.

- **Prepositional**: the learner prefers Stifter's term. Days 001-007 currently say "dative"; switch to "prepositional (called dative in other grammars)" with a one-time note, and revert the table-tool tooltip.
- **Already removed**: "cell", "historical card", "status", "slice".
- **Check and explain at first use** (found in the written lessons): *following-word effects / badges* (L, N, H, 0 are our labels for the mutation of the next word; say "mutation of the next word" and keep the letters as abbreviations), *chain table* (call it "development table: PIE to Old Irish"), *«* (explain once as "replaced by analogy"), *analogy*, *a-affection / i-affection / u-infection* (Stifter's terms; one-line example each), *syncope*, *palatalized / non-palatalized* (Stifter's wording; mention once that older grammars, including Thurneysen, say "slender / broad"), *thematic vowel*, *denominative, causative, iterative* (explain with an example), *hiatus verbs*, *nasal-infix present*, *laryngeal*, *citation form*, *paradigm* (say "the full set of forms").
- **Standard terms that stay**: nominative, genitive, accusative, vocative, lenition, nasalization, aspiration, gemination, absolute / conjunct, deuterotonic / prototonic (Stifter's), preverb, copula, stem class.
- **Avoid in plans and tooltips**: "slice", "pool", "target" (use "topic").

## Policy: explanations that Stifter does not give

When a lesson raises a "why" question that Stifter does not answer:
1. Search other sources first (Thurneysen's *Grammar of Old Irish*, McCone, other textbooks and research papers, standard reference pages) and use an explanation only if a source states it; name the source in the lesson.
2. If a source gives only part of an answer, give that part and say what is still missing.
3. If nothing explains it, say so in plain words ("no source I could consult explains this") and present the point as a rule to memorize. Never invent a reason.
4. Quick-answer questions must be phrased so a beginner can follow them (no jargon without a one-line explanation).

Applied so far: Day 008 (the *-s-* forms of the article). Also applied: Day 009 (why *ind* before *l, r, n, f*: partial, Toner), Day 055 (why the copula's conjunct forms lenite: no source found).

## Policy: abbreviations for morphology terms

Cases, number, gender, person, verb-form names and the grammar words listed in `REFERENCE.md` are written as short standard abbreviations in lessons, titles and tables: nom, gen, prep (Stifter's prepositional; dative, dat, in other grammars), acc, voc; sg, pl; masc, fem, neut; 1sg ... 3pl; abs, conj, deut, prot; pres, pret, impf, fut, cond, ind, subj, impv, pass; depo, caus, iter, denom, vn, rel, poss, dem; pal, npal (palatalized, non-palatalized). Lesson Markdown uses plain lowercase; the site build turns them into small capitals with tooltips and puts the reference panel (abbreviations, marks [L] [N] [H] [0], palatalization) at the top of each lesson. A term is written in full only where a lesson defines it. New lessons must follow `REFERENCE.md`.
